## Rulings on the Escalations

### S1 — WORK-210's shape: the header correction or the out-of-frame bound

**Ruling: the header correction now; the out-of-frame bound deferred with a named, cheap trigger. Both are right and they are right at different times.**

The header correction is XS, lands in the probe, and removes the whole of the live risk — which is that a sentence in a standing record claims more than an instrument can deliver. The bound is S, edits `run.mjs`, and buys something nobody currently needs: the ability to **act** on one of these numbers.

**`run.mjs` is the project's single render runner and all five commands go through it.** It is not a sixth runner and the ceiling is not in question, but it is the one runner that exists, and changing it to serve a probe that gates nothing, asserts nothing and appears in no command is a change to the release path for a benefit that is not yet claimed. **Prefer the smallest change that removes the risk**, and the risk today is a false sentence, not a false number.

**The trigger is precise and it is the standing convention applied to itself:** the first time any figure from `perf.js` is proposed as firing a trigger, closing an item, or discharging a deferral, **WORK-210(b) lands first, before the figure is acted on.** Land tooling before the fix it will verify, invoked for the sixth time. **Shape pre-ruled so it needs no architect round:** Node bounds the whole run in wall time around the existing `spawnSync` at `run.mjs:132-137`, the probe reports the sum of its own measured durations, and a gross disagreement between the two is dilation observed from outside the domain being questioned. Nothing else in `run.mjs` changes, and the bound is reported, never asserted, for the same reason the probe asserts no figure.

### S2 — WORK-213: is a number wanted, and is a cap ever admissible?

**Ruling: no number now; the item is deferred whole. A silent row cap is permanently rejected. Event delegation is admissible in principle and is the pre-ruled shape if the deferral ever fires.**

**On the measurement.** WORK-212 and WORK-213 look like the same XS, and they are not. `renderDaily`'s cost is **filter-independent by design** — `renderCalendar` reads `db.actual` in full — which is the "no escape hatch" exposure property that round 9 treated as the reason to make a trigger cheap to test. The Income and Expenses lists have the escape hatch: narrowing the date filter shrinks them. That is the exact distinction my own standing decision has used for six rounds, and it points different ways for the two items. Measuring a cost the user can already escape, whose fix is M and unruled, produces a number nobody will act on. **Work not done is the cheapest work there is.**

**On the cap.** A list that silently omits records in a personal finance application is not a performance decision, it is a statement to the user about how many transactions they have — and this is an application whose first principle is correctness of financial data and whose audience has no training. **A cap that hides records is off limits, permanently.** Pagination and virtualisation are not rejected but are not pre-ruled either; they change what a list is and they need their own round.

**Event delegation is a different object entirely** — one listener per list container replacing two per row, behaviour-identical, no product decision in it. **That is the pre-ruled shape**, so if the trigger ever fires nobody needs me for it.

### S3 — WORK-212's number and the WORK-16/49 trigger's Analytics half

**Ruling: no, a `renderDaily` figure above 100ms does not fire WORK-16/49 as the trigger is currently written — and rather than leave that as a technicality, I am re-aiming the trigger, because the measurement exposed a defect in my own round-9 wording.**

That reasoning confused **exposure** with **cost**. Narrowing the filter does not stop each month scanning the whole store — true, per scan — but it also shrinks the month list, which is the multiplier. The measurement makes it plain: This Month builds one column and costs 2ms; All Time builds thirty-six and costs 62ms. **I aimed my own trigger at the cheapest configuration and excluded the expensive one, and as written it was unlikely ever to fire.** `HANDOFF.md:303-314` recorded that correctly and correctly declined to act on it. Acting on it is mine.

### S4 — WORK-222: which reconciliation

**Ruling: the deploy trigger becomes deliberate. `push` is removed from `deploy.yml`; `workflow_dispatch` at `:28` becomes the only trigger; and `sw.js:1` gains the derivation rather than an excuse.**

The two candidate reconciliations are not equal, because one of them makes the project's own model of "deploy" true and the other makes a rule optional.

Everything else in this project already treats a deploy as a deliberate publication: WORK-203 was approved as *"one bump per deploy, not one per sprint"*, `HANDOFF.md:55-59` treats the bump as a manual pre-deploy step, and the user's live situation is four files uploaded by hand. **A push trigger would publish every intermediate commit of a sprint to installed users, on an offline-first application whose cache key is a hand-maintained fact.** That is the wrong default independently of the comment, and it is the thing that makes the comment unfollowable.

Rewording `sw.js:1` alone would leave a rule that says "bump before publishing" attached to a pipeline that publishes without asking. **The smallest change that removes the risk is the one that makes the stated rule followable**, and `deploy.yml:28` already provides the mechanism.

**Conditions.** One commit. The comment at `deploy.yml:28` is updated to say it is now the only trigger and why — the cache key is a hand-maintained fact and a publish is a deliberate act. `sw.js:1` states the derivation in the same commit: bump before an intentional publish; the cache key is not a per-commit fact. **The verify gate on the workflow is untouched.** **And this ruling attaches no remote, connects nothing to the user's separate repository, and changes nothing about their manual upload** — that decision remains theirs alone, exactly as `HANDOFF.md:188-201` records.

## Conventions — two added

### C43 — a citation names the thing, not only the place

Derived from S6 and CODE-05, and from the fact that this is the fourth instance in three rounds and the first to survive a correction pass aimed at it.

> **A line number in prose — in a comment, a probe header, a report or an acceptance condition — is decoration. The citation is the selector, the function name or the identifier, and it is written first. Any number written beside one is greped back and read at the moment it is written. An approval that cites a line number is subject to the same rule as one that names an assertion: the author checks it before publishing, not after.**

`HANDOFF.md:99-123` already diagnoses this class correctly as a habit, and the diagnosis did not close the case it was written from — because a habit binds whoever remembers it and a convention binds the author of a condition. The rule is cheap, needs no instrument, and is off the table as a fifth static predicate: the ceiling is four plus one and it holds. **I applied C43 to this document before publishing it, and it is how I established that CODE-05's enumeration is complete.**

### C44 — an instrument that cannot bound its own domain may hold a deferral, but may not fire a trigger

Derived from C4, and it is the general form of the thing that makes this round's headline finding tractable rather than paralysing.

> **A measurement whose instrument cannot establish that its units are real may be used as corroborating evidence for a deferral that already stands, because a deferral standing is the default state and needs no evidence. It may NOT be used to fire a trigger, close an item, discharge a deferral, or schedule work — because the dilation it cannot detect distorts in both directions, and a figure above a threshold is no more trustworthy than one below it. When such a figure is first proposed for any of those purposes, the instrument's domain is bounded from outside first, and the figure is re-taken under the bound.**

This is C34 read one level down. C34 says a measurement trigger is discharged only by a measurement; C44 says what a measurement has to be able to say about itself before it counts as one. It is also the reason WORK-210(b) is a deferral with a sharp trigger rather than either an approval or a rejection: **the instrument's trust only has to be paid for on the day a decision turns on it, and that day names itself.**
