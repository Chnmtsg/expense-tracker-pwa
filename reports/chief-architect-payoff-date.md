## Verified Against Source, Not Accepted On Report

- **§3's table is true in every row, and I checked each at source rather than from the report.** `.debt-rate` carries its own hoisted comment closing the rate copy at one sentence permanently. `.debt-rate.ask` is gated on `costAgreed > 0 && debtTermDays(d) === null` and asks for the due date. `.debt-meta` renders at most four pills — borrowed-on, the due chip, "Cost so far", the note chip. `.debt-totals` renders four tiles, the fourth gated on `showCost`. The action row carries five controls. **The request did not overstate one surface.**
- **The card foot's invariant is real and it is by construction, not by convention.** Three gated `.helper` lines: `payments === 0`, `overpaid > 0`, `settledShort > 0`. `overpaid` is `Math.max(0, paid − total)` and is zero whenever `payments === 0`; `settledShort` requires `payments > 0` and `outstanding > 0`, which cannot hold while `overpaid > 0`. **At most one renders in every reachable state, and the exclusivity is arithmetic rather than discipline.** `PDK5` corrects how the request cited it.
- **A payoff date cannot join that foot, and this is the fact that decides three of §3's five shapes.** A scheduled debt with no payments recorded renders *"No payments recorded yet."* A fourth gated line would render beside it. `SAV-01`'s rule is that **a gated line on a card must prove exclusivity or it does not ship**, and this one provably cannot. The refusal is arithmetic, not taste.
- **`stepDate`'s monthly branch is exactly as §2 describes it**, including the comment recording the `setMonth()` overflow, the 31st clamp and *"Two engines, one defect, twice."* It takes `anchorDay` and clamps to the month's own length. **A walk that does not go through it reproduces a defect this repository has paid for twice**, and the arithmetic differs: 31 Jan stepped twice through `stepDate` with anchor 31 gives **2026-03-31**; the naive `setMonth` walk gives 3 April. That divergence is the fixture this item is owed.
- **`debtTermDays` measures `date` to `dueDate` and nothing else**, its comment states that it is one rule with two readers, and `debtAnnualCostRate` consumes it. **A schedule is a second, better term source and wiring it in would silently re-term a figure already on users' screens.** §4(c) is right to want it refused by name. `PDR6`.
- **`debtAnnualCostRate`'s comment already anticipates this day** — *"it needs a repayment schedule, this application records payments as they happen and no schedule at all… The standing decision names the day that becomes computable."* **That sentence is now false at source and is not this item's to correct.** The effective rate is its own request; correcting that comment belongs to it, not here. `PDN6` records it so it is not swept.
- **`debtSettled(d)` is shape-tested with the failure direction "not settled", and the rate line renders on a cleared card by design** — *"The rate is a property of the contract, so it stays true after the debt is settled."* That shipped precedent decides half of §4(d). `PDK7`.
- **`firstDue` is the one schedule field with no guard of its own, and this is the ground that carries the item.** The save branch refuses a partial schedule, a count below two, a first date before the borrow date, and instalments that miss the agreed total by a whole instalment. **The sum rule guards `instalment` and `count` together. Nothing guards the date.** `firstDue` is deliberately not cross-checked against `dueDate`, `debtProblem` checks only its shape, and a `firstDue` of 2027-02-01 on a 2026 debt stores silently and correctly. **`SCD2` and the effective rate will both read it.**
- **`openDebtEditModal`'s comment became a rule at `SCH-02` and the rule now reads *"Nothing derived, nothing about conduct, no status and no history."*** A computed line in that modal contradicts the sentence as written. **Amending it is a binding condition of this item, not a tidy-up** — see `PDT-01`(7).
- **`feeReportWorking` is the shape precedent and it is in the file**: a form-time line, `class="helper"`, `role="status"`, `aria-live="polite"`, hidden with `display:none` when there is nothing to state, written with `textContent` and never `innerHTML`, copy closed at one sentence permanently. **A second dialect for the same object would be one to learn for no reason.**
- **`.debt-totals`' "CLOSED AT THREE SENTENCES. One ungated, two gated" counts sentences and not elements, and it is accurate** — the gated helper carries two sentences in one element. Recorded because it reads like a stale tally and is not one; nobody is to "correct" a true comment. `PDN4`.

## Rulings on the Request's Six Questions

### Q1 (§3) — where the figure lands

**(d). Inside the edit modal, beside the three fields that produce it, as a form-time line that stores nothing, fills nothing and touches no card surface. (a), (b), (c) and (e) are each refused, and (e) is refused on its merits rather than by default — I took it seriously and it loses to one fact the request did not put on its own list.**

**Why not (e), which is the answer I have to justify first.** The request offers me refusal and disclaims the sunk-cost argument, correctly. I am not using it: **`PDT-01` would be approved on the same ground if `SCH-01` had never shipped, because the ground is not that storage was bought — it is what the storage is missing.** The save branch refuses four things about a schedule and **`firstDue` is not one of them.** `debtProblem` tests its shape and explicitly does not cross-check it. `SCH-02` refuses only a first payment earlier than the borrow date, and a refusal against `dueDate` was ruled out for a good reason that still holds. So a user who types 2027 for 2026, or picks the wrong month in a native date control on a phone, stores a date that is wrong by a year and **no surface in this application will ever disagree with it.** `SCD2` will ring a bell off it. The effective rate will term a percentage off it. **The smallest thing that removes that risk is showing the user, at the moment they type it, what it implies — a date they can check against the paper in their hand.** That is not a feature; it is the guard the write path is missing. Refusing it means accepting an unchecked date in a financial record, and correctness of financial data outranks everything, including my preference for doing nothing.

**Why (d) and not the four alternatives, in one line each.**

**(a) — displacing the due chip. Refused permanently, `PDR1`.** The request's reading is right and it is `SCR15`'s shape with a new subject: a stated fact hidden behind a derived one. `dueDate` is a term of the contract the user typed; a computed date replacing it would mean the card stops printing something the user said in order to print something the application worked out. It would also silently kill the urgency states — `overdue Nd`, `due today` — which the bell agrees with.

**(b) — a fifth `.debt-meta` pill. Refused, `PDR2`**, `SCR10` and `DR2` carried without re-argument: a figure that needs a sentence does not land in a pill. The request listed it so the rejection stands against this item rather than only against the last one. It now does.

**(c) — spending the gated ask line. Refused permanently, `PDR3`**, and the request's own analysis is the reason plus one more. The two states overlap — a debt with a schedule, a cost and no due date is an ordinary record — so the card would have to choose between asking for something and telling the user something. **And the choice is worse than it looks in one direction: suppressing the ask to show the payoff date means the user is never asked for the due date, so the rate line the module exists to produce never becomes computable.** A slot that asks for an input is not a slot that states an output.

**(d) — the edit modal. Approved, with conditions, as `PDT-01`.** It touches no closed surface, it makes no claim on the card, and the figure appears exactly where its inputs are typed. **Its cost is `SCN7`, and I am paying it with my eyes open** — see Q5.

### Q2 (§4(a)) — `dueDate` against the computed last instalment

**Shown, not refused and not reconciled — and under (d) the "if both render" in §4(a) is answered by the shape: they render on exactly one surface, the edit modal, where both are the user's own typed fields and both are editable in place.**

**Not refused.** A save-time cross-check is a rule change to a shipped write path, and it protects nothing. `SCH-02`(4) ruled that `firstDue` is not cross-checked against `dueDate` because *"a refusal that protects nothing costs a legitimate record"*, and the same sentence governs the other end of the schedule. A contract whose last instalment falls after the stated due-by date is a real contract — a due date renegotiated, a due date typed as the date of the first payment, a lender's paper carrying both — and an application that refused to record it would be deciding which of two facts the user stated about their own loan is the true one. **`PDR8`.**

**Not reconciled.** No sentence, anywhere, explaining the difference. `SCR16`'s reasoning does not transfer unexamined — there the two figures never met on a screen and the sentence would have created the contradiction it explained; here they do meet, in the modal. So I rule it on its merits and the answer is the same: **the application does not referee between two things the user stated about their own contract.** A sentence saying *"this is later than the date you gave above"* is advice, this module gives none, and the user is looking at both fields with the cursor in one of them. **`PDR9`.**

**Shown.** The line states the last instalment date and says nothing about the due date. The due date field is six inches above it. **That is the only surface in this application where the disagreement is both visible and fixable, and it is now the surface the figure renders on** — which is a better outcome than the one §4(a) feared and it falls out of Q1 rather than being engineered.

### Q3 (§4(c)) — the shipped rate's term source

**Untouched. Refused by name, permanently, and thank you for asking it rather than leaving it unproposed.**

> **`debtTermDays` measures `date` to `dueDate` and will go on doing so. The schedule is not a term source for it, for `debtAnnualCostRate`, or for any figure that renders on a card, a tile or the Dashboard. `PDR6`.**

Three grounds, any one sufficient. **First, it would change a live number with no line in any commit message saying so** — the rate is on users' screens at v19, and a two-line "tidy-up" that re-terms it is the quietest way this module could ship a wrong figure. **Second, it is the third term derivation refused at `ER7`**, in a module whose two existing ones carry a comment warning the next reader against unifying them. **Third, and decisively: a term derived from a schedule is a different quantity.** `debtAnnualCostRate` is deliberately a property of the **contracted span**, constant for the life of the debt; a schedule-derived term would make the same label mean something else on records that happen to carry a schedule and the old thing on records that do not. **One label, two derivations, chosen by the presence of an optional field, is the defect this module's own rate comment exists to prevent.**

**The effective rate is a separate item, it is fireable today, it is not fired here, and it arrives as its own scoped request.** When it does, it is a **new, labelled figure** that must argue for itself; it is not `debtAnnualCostRate` quietly re-termed.

### Q4 (§4(e)) — the reader's shape, against `SCH-01`(3)

**A named function, and `SCH-01`(3) is not strained by it, because dead code means a function with NO reader and this one has a reader. One caller is a caller.**

**But the function is not the one the question imagines, and correcting that is the useful half of this answer.** The reader does not take a debt record. **It takes `{ count, firstDue }` and ignores everything else, because the payoff date does not depend on the instalment at all** — it is `firstDue` stepped `count − 1` times, and the instalment is not in that expression. One argument shape serves both paths: the modal builds it from the three fields as the user types, and those fields are filled from `d.schedule` when the modal opens, so **there is one code path, one reader and no second entry point.** The comment states that the figure does not depend on the instalment, as a rule, so nobody later wires it in.

**Shape-defensive, with the failure direction "no schedule", exactly as `PD1`'s row requires.** It returns `null` — never a throw, never a guess, never a partial date — unless `count` is an integer of at least one and `firstDue` matches `ISO_DATE_RE`. It re-checks rather than trusting the validator, because `debtProblem` has never run over records arriving through `loadFromCloud` (`WORK-13`), and `openDebtEditModal` already writes a cloud record's schedule straight into the fields.

**Where it lives: beside the form-time code it serves, on `feeComputeTotal`'s precedent, and NOT in the derived-figures block.** `SCH-01`(3) said that block's header states everything in it is a stock as of now, and that clause is upheld rather than argued around. A payoff date is not a card figure and must not be made to look like one by its address in the file.

**And why a function rather than a clause inside the handler, which is the real question.** Two reasons, and the second is the one that decides it. Inline, the defensive contract is invisible — a reader of the handler sees input parsing, not a guarded derivation with a stated failure direction. And inline, **the failure direction cannot be asserted**: the harness can call `debtPayoffDate({count, firstDue})` with a malformed object exactly as it calls `debtProblem` and `debtSettled` today, and if the guard lives inside an event handler the only way to test it is to drive a modal. **A guard nothing can point an assertion at is a guard on trust.**

### Q5 (§4(f)) — discoverability, and `SCN7`

**Answered "no, and deliberately", written down here so the next round finds a decision rather than an oversight.**

**`SCN7` stands, unchanged and now paid for twice: a user cannot tell from the Debts screen which debts carry a schedule, and after this item they still cannot.** The only visible consequence of the whole schedule feature lives behind ✎. That is the price of keeping four closed surfaces closed, and it is a price and not a free choice — I am recording it as such rather than presenting (d) as costless.

**`SCN7`'s fence is reaffirmed: it is not to be fixed by a chip.** Nor by a fourth gated foot line, which provably cannot prove exclusivity; nor by a fifth pill; nor by a fifth tile; nor by displacing the due chip. **Every shape available on that card today is refused, which is exactly why I am not pre-ruling the answer.** It becomes `PDD1`, deferred with an observation trigger, and the observation has to describe the need before a shape can be chosen for it. **A shape invented now, against no observation, on a card with nothing free, would be the speculation this ruling exists to refuse.**

### Q6 (§4(b) and §4(d)) — confirmations

**§4(b) — `dueDate` is not made redundant. Confirmed, permanently, and dropping either field is refused by name at `PDR7`.** The request's reason is right and there are two more. They answer different questions: `dueDate` is when the whole obligation falls due, the last instalment is when the last agreed payment falls, and on an ordinary contract they are the same day — which is a coincidence of that contract, not an identity. **`dueDate` is load-bearing in three places a schedule does not reach:** it is the sole term source for `debtTermDays` and therefore for the shipped rate line; it drives the due chip and its urgency states; and `computeReminders` is keyed on it. **Dropping it would silently remove a rate from every card and a reminder from every bell.** And the reverse proposal — dropping the schedule because a due date exists — is refused by `SCK4`'s own sentence: `dueDate` is a schedule with a count of one, and one number is not the other.

**§4(d) — settled debts. Confirmed on the card, the tiles and the bell; narrowed inside the modal, deliberately and with a reason.**

**Confirmed, and it costs nothing, because under this ruling the payoff date reaches no card, no tile, no chip and no bell item for any debt, settled or live.** The `debtSettled` gate is nonetheless stated as a standing fence rather than left implicit: **if a payoff date is ever ruled onto a card, a tile or the bell, it gates on `debtSettled(d)` before anything else** — a finished debt is not chased about a future payment.

**Narrowed inside the modal, and this is mine rather than the request's.** The line is **not** suppressed for a settled debt, and the module's own shipped precedent is why: `debtAnnualCostRate`'s line renders on a cleared card, on the stated ground that *"the rate is a property of the contract, so it stays true after the debt is settled."* **A payoff date is the same class of object, and the modal is where the terms of a contract are stated, not where obligations are asserted.** Suppressing it would mean a user correcting a settled debt's schedule types into a form that has stopped answering. **The gate binds readers that assert an obligation; it does not bind a line that states a term.** `PDK7`.

**And `SCR15` is reaffirmed at full width: settling a debt never deletes, blanks or hides the schedule.** Nothing here touches `settledOn`, `debtSettled`, or the settle sheet.

## §6 — What the request is not asking for

**Accepted in full, every clause onto the off-limits list, and three of them enforced rather than merely accepted.** Not a saving — `PR1`, permanent, and the request is right that a schedule does not make one computable because the agreed total is still fixed at agreement. Not an ordering. Not a projection over multiple records, not a total payoff date across all debts, not a countdown, not a progress figure against the schedule (`SCR6`), not an instalment ledger (`SCR5`), not a reminder (`SCD2`, its own request), not an effective rate, not a new screen, modal, sheet or `editCtx.kind`, not a fifth tile, not a sixth control, no change to `debtOutstanding`, `debtPaid`, `debtInterestPaid`, `debtSettled`, `debtAnnualCostRate`, `debtTermDays` or any write path, and **no new stored field — this figure is computed and never saved.**

**Enforced rather than accepted: the countdown, the total across debts and the stored end date.** They are on the rejection table with IDs, because a disclaimer in a request binds the request and not the future — **sixth outing of that clarification**, and it will be quoted at `PDT-01` by somebody who reads §6 and not this ruling.

**One clause needs its ground stated, because it is the one that will be argued.** A countdown — *"eleven months to go"*, *"free by June"* — is refused permanently at `PDR5` and **not** because the arithmetic is unavailable. It is refused because the date is a **term** and the countdown is a **promise**: it asserts that the remaining instalments will be paid, which is conduct the application has never observed, and it delivers it as encouragement to a user under financial stress. `PR2`'s whole reasoning, arriving in miniature. **The date says the loop has an end; the countdown says the user will reach it, and only one of those is in the record.**

## Conflict Rulings

**There is no Engineering Manager report and therefore no recorded conflict list, and no reviewer findings this cycle.** These are the seven collisions I found between the request, the standing record and the source. Each is ruled.

### PDK1 — "there is deliberately no calculator in the edit modal" against a computed line in the edit modal

**Ruling: the distinction holds, the ban is reaffirmed at full width, and I am stating the difference precisely because this is the sentence that will be quoted against `PDT-01`.** `FR6` refused a **rate input in the edit modal that recomputes `totalToRepay`** — a calculator that *writes a stored field* from an input the record does not keep, producing a total nothing can ever recompute. **`PDT-01` writes no field, fills no input, stores nothing, and can be overridden by nobody because it is not an input.** Every one of its inputs is already stored on the record.

**And the fee narrowing is neither invoked nor widened, which matters more than it looks.** That narrowing permits *a rate the user states, consumed by a form-time calculator that **fills an existing field**, stores nothing, shows its working, and can be overridden by typing.* **`PDT-01` falls outside it rather than inside it, because it does not fill a field** — it is a strictly smaller object than the one already permitted. `feeReportWorking` is cited here as a **shape precedent for how a form-time line is built**, not as a grant of permission. **The narrowing stands at exactly the width it was granted. Nobody is to read this ruling as having opened it.**

### PDK2 — "this application does not project" against printing a future date

**Ruling: the rule is about forecasting conduct, and it is untouched.** `PAY-01`'s strategy item reads that *a figure whose truth depends on conduct that has not happened is a forecast.* **The truth of this date depends on no conduct whatsoever** — it is `firstDue` plus `count − 1` months, and it is exactly as true if the user pays nothing. `dueDate` has been a stored, printed future date since the module's second round, and the card prints `12d left` against it. **The module has printed a future date the user stated since long before this request; what it has never printed is a future date the application inferred, and it still does not.** The line where projection would begin is the countdown, and that is why `PDR5` exists.

### PDK3 — `SCR9` against this item

**Ruling: `SCR9` bound `SCH-01` and `SCH-02`, it did its job, and it does not bind here.** Its own words were *"`PD1` is not fired by this ruling and is not fired by approval. It is fired by `SCH-02` reaching `main`"* — and `SCH-02` is on `main` at `a7b6edf` and live at v19, verified at source. **Its second sentence is now settled rather than pending: a last instalment date is a payoff date with a different label, both labels name this figure, and it ships once, under `PD1`'s name, as `PDT-01`.** Nobody may later propose "the last instalment date" as a second item; it is this one wearing the other label, and it is already built.

### PDK4 — `SCH-01`(3)'s no-dead-code rule against a function with one caller

**Ruling: the rule said what it said and it is not strained.** *"Nothing reads the field in this commit, and a function with no reader is dead code."* **Dead code is code with no reader. One reader is a reader.** The module's own extraction precedent — `debtTermDays`, extracted because *"is there a term is ONE rule with two readers"* — argues for plurality, and I am ruling against it here for a reason that precedent did not face: **the failure direction has to be assertable.** A guard folded into an event handler can only be exercised by driving a modal; a named function is called by the harness with a malformed object exactly as `debtProblem` and `debtSettled` are called today. **The clause of `SCH-01`(3) that does still bind — no entry into the derived-figures block — is upheld, not argued around.**

### PDK5 — §3's citation of `tools\harness\debts.js:1804`

**Ruling: the property is true, the citation is the wrong shape, and the correction is worth two lines because this repository has paid for it before.** The exclusivity is real and it is **arithmetic, not convention** — I verified the three gates at source and they cannot co-occur. What the harness does is narrower than the request's phrasing: the flow *"a settled debt says what it left unpaid, and only a settled one"* asserts the helper count over **five enumerated states**, not over "any reachable state" universally. **And the citation names a line number, which `ARCH-01` and `C43` both forbid: lead with the flow name, because the number is stale the moment anything above it moves.** `HANDOFF.md` carries a worked example of that exact rot. **Nothing is spent either way, because no card line ships.**

### PDK6 — §7's argument against §3's own conclusion

**Ruling: the request put its strongest argument nowhere, and its weakest one last.** §7 offers the mission payload — *"the last one falls on this day"* is the single sentence that says the loop ends — and correctly identifies it as the argument that would justify spending a closed surface. **No closed surface is being spent, so that argument is not what carries this item.** What carries it is on neither list: **`firstDue` is unguarded, and a form-time line is the only guard that costs nothing.** That reframes `PD1` from a display feature into a write-path guard, and it is why the shape the request held **weakly** is the one I hold firmly. **Second consecutive ruling in this module where the decisive fact was found by reading the write path rather than the report** — and the first where it turned a modest feature into a necessary one.

### PDK7 — §4(d)'s settled-debt gate against the rate line on a cleared card

**Ruling: confirmed where it protects something, narrowed where it would only silence a form, and the shipped file supplied the precedent.** The schedule ruling said every future reader of the schedule gates on `debtSettled(d)` first. **That fence exists so no surface asserts an obligation the user has finished** — the bell, the card, the tiles. **It was never a rule that a finished contract stops having terms**, and the file already says so: the rate line renders on a cleared card because *"the rate is a property of the contract"*, and `SCR15` forbids destroying a stated fact to tidy a display. **So: gated on every card, tile and bell item, permanently and in advance; not gated inside the modal, where the subject is the contract and not the obligation.**

## Rulings on What the Request Did Not Ask

**PDN1 — the walk is unbounded and the modal can reach it.** `debtProblem` accepts any integer `count` of one or more; `loadFromCloud` never runs `debtProblem` (`WORK-13`); `openDebtEditModal` writes `String(d.schedule.count)` straight into the field. **A count of one million freezes the tab in a loop.** Fenced at `PDT-01`(3) with a stated ceiling on `recInterval`'s own idiom — *beyond fifty years of monthly payments it is a typo, not a schedule* — returning `null` rather than walking, and stated as a rule rather than a tally. **This is the one genuinely new hazard in the item and it costs three lines.**

**PDN4 — `.debt-totals`' "CLOSED AT THREE SENTENCES. One ungated, two gated" is accurate and reads like a stale tally.** It counts **sentences**, and the gated helper carries two of them in one element. **Recorded so the next reader does not "correct" a true comment into a false one** — the inverse of `SK6`, and the first time that direction has come up.

**PDN5 — the instalment field gains no listener and the figure never depends on it.** Stated as a rule in the reader's comment, not as a note, because the obvious "improvement" is to recompute on all three fields and the second obvious one is to print the instalment beside the date.

## Architecture Strategy — next quarter

**What stays, and is not open for discussion.** Everything in the standing record and all nine design-request rulings, **with no entry narrowed by this document.** The single self-contained `expense-pwa\index.html` that runs by being opened from disk. No framework, no build step, no bundler, no component abstraction, **no new dependency — reaffirmed against this request specifically: no date library and no schedule library, because `stepDate` is the one definition of a recurrence step and a second one has cost this repository twice.** `localStorage`, one write seam, `load()` total, quarantine before write, numbered append-only migrations. **Offline-first, mobile-first, and correctness of financial data above everything** — and this request is decided by that last clause twice: once in approving a line whose job is to catch a wrong stored date, and once in refusing to re-term a percentage already on users' screens. A debt figure never reaches a period-filtered surface (`C38`). A collection that must never reach a total keeps its own array (`C39`). A figure the application computed by a model of its own is labelled as such (`C36`). Comments state rules, never tallies (`ARCH-01`). A citation names the thing, not the place (`C43`). **The record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it.**

**What changes.**

1. **A derived figure may render at the boundary where its inputs are typed, and that is not a display feature — it is a guard.** Where a stored field has no refusal of its own, a form-time line stating what it implies is the cheapest check available and the only one that costs no surface. **This is the rule that decided §3 and it is the most portable thing in this ruling.**
2. **A closed surface is not an argument against a feature. It is an argument about where the feature goes.** Five shapes were offered; the one that touched nothing closed was also the one that did the most work. **A request that opens "there is nowhere to put this" has usually not finished looking.**
3. **The `debtSettled` gate binds readers that assert an obligation, not readers that state a term.** The rate line on a cleared card was always the worked example, and it is now the rule.
4. **A deferral that fires is discharged, not re-tabled.** `PD1` becomes an item and leaves the deferred table; its unanswered clause becomes a new deferral with its own trigger rather than surviving inside a discharged row. **A row that is half-built and half-pending is how a fence turns into a habit.**
5. **A shape is not pre-ruled when every shape available is already refused.** `PDD1` carries a trigger and no answer, deliberately: an observation has to describe the need before a surface is spent on it.

**Off limits this quarter — five additions:** a payoff date on any card, tile, chip or bell item; a countdown, a progress figure or a "free by" in any form; the schedule as a term source for `debtTermDays` or the shipped rate line; a stored, cross-checked or refused end date; and any sentence reconciling `dueDate` against the last instalment.

**Risks recorded, not scheduled. None is a finding and no reviewer raised any of them.**

- **Mine, and the largest.** **The discoverability answer is still nothing, and this is the second consecutive ruling that accepts it.** A user who never opens ✎ sees no consequence of a feature they typed three numbers into. `PDD1` carries it with a trigger; it does not remove it.
- **Mine, and the sharpest.** **The line is feedback, not a refusal.** It shows a wrong `firstDue`; it does not stop one. A user who does not read it stores exactly what they would have stored before, and `SCD2` and the effective rate will both read it. **The alternative — refusing a schedule whose dates disagree with the due date — costs legitimate records and was refused at `PDR8`, so this is the guard that exists rather than the guard that is strongest.**
- **Mine, from `PDK1`.** **The edit modal is now the door for two features and states one derived figure**, and the next request that wants a door will point at it. `openDebtEditModal`'s amended rule — no derived **field**, a line that states what the stated terms imply is not a field — is the whole of what was granted, and it is one good argument away from a second derived line.
- **Mine, from `PDN1`.** **The border and the boundary disagree about `count` on purpose, and the gap is now walked by a loop.** The ceiling closes it. If anyone ever removes the ceiling because "the validator already checks that", it does not.
- **Carried and unchanged.** The instalment field is one digit away from a wrong record and the sum rule is the only guard. Monthly is baked into the schedule's shape rather than a field. "Cost so far" still overstates an early settlement. A backup restored by an older build loses `settledOn` and `schedule`. No archive on the Debts screen. `totalToRepay` has no edit trail. Every string is a literal and the language layer still needs its own ruling. Six bell write sites. `renderDebts` walks the payment ledger roughly seven times per debt and the pre-ruled one-`Map` fix stays unscheduled — **and `PDT-01` does not fire it, because nothing in this item runs inside `renderDebts`.** The add form stands at ten controls and the action row at five; **this request adds to neither, deliberately.**
