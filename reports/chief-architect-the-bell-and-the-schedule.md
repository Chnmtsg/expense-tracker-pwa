## The ruling in one screen

1. **§3 is ruled as the request asks — the next occurrence at or after today — and it is ruled on a ground stronger than the request's own.** The request argues that nothing can clear an overdue instalment. True, and `SCR5` says so by name. But the decisive sentence is already in the shipped file, in the debt branch's own comment: *"a reminder that cannot be acted on is the reminder `nextPlannedDue` was rewritten to stop firing."* **The rule was in the file and the request did not cite it.**
2. **§4(a) is displacement, and I am saying the word.** But it is displacement **in one direction only**: the slot holds the **nearer** of the two dates the user stated. A schedule can make the bell speak sooner. **It can never make the bell speak later or go quiet**, and that is arithmetic rather than discipline — `min(a, b) <= cutoff` whenever `a <= cutoff`.
3. **§4(b) rides `showDebts`, and the request's own worry dissolves.** Under (2) there is no second item to toggle. A setting that switched the schedule half off would be a setting that makes one item report a **later** date than the one the application holds. That is not a preference; it is a defect with a checkbox.
4. **§4(c) is the shared ceiling, the shared engine, no measurement owed.** `computeReminders` already calls `nextPlannedDue` per planned record on every bell update under a **20,000**-step guard, and the debt branch already filters the whole payment ledger twice per debt through `debtSettled`. A 600-step walk, entered only for a **live, scheduled** debt, is smaller than both and sits in the same function.
5. **§4(d) is confirmed, and it stops being a special case.** An exhausted schedule yields no date, so the slot holds `dueDate` and the item is byte-identical to today's. It falls out of (2) rather than being handled.
6. **§4(e) is ruled, `title` included, and closed permanently on the way in.**
7. **§5.7 — it ships, and the owner is asked first.** It ships because the shipped comment already states the rule that admits it: *"a due date is a DATED COMMITMENT, not money spent."* An agreed instalment is the same object on the same record. The owner is asked first because this is the first thing in the whole schedule arc that reaches a user **who is not looking at the screen.**
8. **`EFD1` fires on the day this lands, by its own words, and it is not built here.** `firstDue` acquires its third reader. That is a separate change with separate harms and it is its own scoped request.

## §3 — the question `SCD2` was deferred on

> **Ruled: the next occurrence at or after today. An instalment that fell due and was not paid is not something this application is in a position to notice, and that is a rule rather than a gap. Permanently. `BLR1`.**

**Four grounds, and the first is the file's own.**

**First, and decisive, the shipped comment already says it.** The debt branch's hoisted comment ends with the sentence this whole request was written to ask: *"a reminder that cannot be acted on is the reminder `nextPlannedDue` was rewritten to stop firing."* An overdue instalment is exactly that item. `SCR5` refuses a ledger, a paid mark and a cursor, permanently and by name, so **nothing in this record can ever clear it**; it would sit unchanged until the whole debt was settled, months later, whatever the user did. **The request found the right answer and the right reason. The file had already written the reason down, and quoting it is worth more than restating it, because a rule that is already in the code is a rule the next reader meets without reading this report.**

**Second, and this is mine: the request's reading of `SCR6` is wrong, and it matters that it is wrong in the request's favour.** §3 supposes that *"a payment you agreed to fell due on 15 August"* slips past `SCR6` on the payoff date's distinction, because it is a fact about the agreement rather than a claim about conduct. **It does not slip past, and the payoff-date distinction does not transfer.** `PDK2` ruled that a payoff date is admissible because *"its truth depends on no conduct whatsoever — it is exactly as true if the user pays nothing."* That test works for a **statement**. **A reminder is not a statement. It is a prompt to act, and a prompt about a payment that may already have been made is a claim that it has not been.** The test for an item on a notification surface is not whether the sentence is true; it is what the item **implies** by being there. **That is a new statement of an old rule and it is the most portable thing in this ruling.**

**Third, the symmetry with the module that has a cursor.** `plannedOccurrencePaid`'s comment records that even with `recLastDone` the cursor **over-reports**, and that the interface therefore says *"paid through"* rather than claiming each occurrence was ticked. **The planned module owns a cursor and still refuses to claim per-occurrence knowledge. The Debts module owns none.** It cannot make the stronger claim on the weaker record.

**Fourth, the product ground, and it is the one the target user feels.** `product-strategy.md` defines the user as *"that user under financial stress, which makes the rule stricter rather than looser."* An item that says a payment was missed, that the user cannot dismiss, that is wrong for everyone who paid on time, and that stays for months, is this application telling a person in a debt spiral that they are failing — **on no evidence, with no way to be corrected.** `PR2`'s reasoning arriving on a surface that follows the user out of the app.

**What would clear it, since the request asked and deserves the answer rather than a refusal.** **Nothing could, and that is the whole reason I am not ruling it.** The only mechanisms that could are the three `SCR5` refuses by name — a per-instalment ledger, a paid mark, a cursor — and a fourth the request did not name, which I am refusing pre-emptively because it is what would arrive as the fix: **inferring that an instalment was paid because a payment of roughly its size appears in `db.debtPayments` since its date.** That is `SCR2`'s shape with a new subject — the application observing conduct and calling the observation a fact — and it is worse here than there, because it would make a notification appear and disappear on arithmetic the user cannot see. **`BLR7`.**

**And the request's closing claim is accepted and recorded as correct.** *"The reason not to is not that it would be untrue. It is that the application would have no way to stop saying it."* **That sentence is the best thing in the request and it is what §3 is ruled on.**

## §4(a) — displacement, ruled explicitly, and bounded

> **Ruled: DISPLACEMENT. One item per debt, keyed `debt:<id>` as today. Where a debt has both a schedule and a due date, the item states the NEARER of the two dates, and the due-date item is displaced only by a date earlier than its own. Never two items. Never a later date. `BLR2`.**

**The fence's two clauses admit exactly two shapes and the request enumerated them correctly. I am taking the first, saying the word, and adding the bound that makes it safe — because the request's shape, taken literally, is unsafe in a way it did not see.**

**What the request's shape would do.** *"The schedule item takes the slot"* means a scheduled debt shows its next instalment whatever the due date says. **`PDR8` permits a contract whose last instalment falls after its stated due-by date** — deliberately, because refusing it costs legitimate records. So a debt whose due date is **three months overdue** and whose next agreed instalment is **next month** would stop showing the overdue prompt and start showing a future one. **The application would learn more about a debt and go quieter about it.** That is the one direction a financial application may never move, and it is the direction `C1` closed permanently and `SR6` refuses for `debtInterestPaid`.

**What is ruled instead, and it is smaller than the alternative rather than larger.** The item's date is the earlier of the two dates the user stated, and the existing cutoff test runs against it. The safety is arithmetic: **`min(a, b) <= cutoff` holds whenever `a <= cutoff`.** So **storing a schedule can remove no bell item that would otherwise render**, can never move an item later, and can only ever make the bell speak sooner. **That property is provable in one line and does not depend on anyone remembering it** — the same class of guarantee as `SAV-01`'s helper-line exclusivity, which `PDR4` established must be arithmetic and not discipline.

**And this is where "not silently" is discharged, on the fence's own stated worry.** Q4's sentence was: *"one key holding two different facts on different days is how a user stops trusting the bell."* **The worry is real and it is disarmed by the label, not by the key.** The two facts are labelled differently in the `title`, which is the line a user reads first, and both carry their date in the `sub`. **What the fence forbids is one sentence meaning two things. What ships is two sentences, each meaning one thing, in one slot.** A key is an identity, not a claim — `planned:<id>` has held a different occurrence on different days since before the Debts module existed, for exactly this reason.

**Two items per debt is refused, and not on taste.** The badge is `reminders.length`; two items would double one debt's weight in a count the user reads as "things to do". `maybeFireOSNotifications` reports *"N urgent items"* off the same array. `openNotifModal` would render the same lender's name twice with two money figures three days apart. And a second item needs a second key, which is a second namespace entry for one record. **`BLR2`.**

## §4(b) — `showDebts`, and no second setting

> **Ruled: it rides `showDebts`. No second checkbox, no sub-toggle, no nested control, no per-schedule preference. `BLR3`.**

**The request offered the right conclusion with the weaker of two reasons, and its own stated worry is dissolved by §4(a) rather than accepted as a cost.**

The request's reason — *"a settings screen growing a row per feature"* — is true and is not what decides it. **What decides it is that under §4(a) there is no second item to toggle.** There is one item per debt, in one slot, stating the nearer of two dates the user typed. **A setting that turned "the monthly ones" off would be a setting that makes that item report a date the application knows is not the next one.** A control whose effect is to degrade the accuracy of a single statement is not a preference; it is a defect with a checkbox on it.

**So the request's fear — *"a user who wants due-date reminders and not monthly ones has no way to say so"* — is refused rather than conceded, because the thing they would be turning off does not exist as a separate object.** What they would actually be asking for is *"remind me later than you could"*, and no other reminder in this application offers that.

**What a user can still do, stated so the cost is visible:** turn `showDebts` off, which takes the due-date reminders with it. **That is a real cost and it is `BLD1`**, deferred with an observation trigger rather than answered with a control nobody has asked for.

## §4(c) — the walk's cost and its ceiling

> **Ruled: the same ceiling, `DEBT_SCHEDULE_MONTHS_MAX`; the same engine, `stepDate`'s monthly branch; no second constant, no second walk, no date library, no arithmetic shortcut. A bounded 600-step walk per live scheduled debt per bell update is acceptable, and no measurement is owed before it. `BLR11`.**

**The request was right not to claim it is free and right to ask. The answer is at source and needed no instrument.**

**First, the bell already does a much larger walk, in this function, today.** `computeReminders`' planned branch calls `nextPlannedDue(p)` for **every** `db.planned` record on **every** bell update, and that walk's guard is **20,000 steps**. Nobody measured that either. **A 600-bounded walk is not a new class of cost in this function; it is a smaller instance of one already there.**

**Second, the ledger is the dominant term and this item does not touch it.** The debt branch already calls `debtSettled(d)`, which calls `debtOutstanding`, which calls `debtPaid`, which **filters the whole payment ledger** — per debt, per bell update. `ORD-01`'s ruling established that the ledger walk is this module's real cost, and recorded that `renderDebts` walks it roughly seven times per debt. **A 600-step date walk against an in-memory integer is not what will make this slow, and the pre-ruled one-`Map` fix stays unscheduled — this item does not fire it.**

**Third, and this is binding because it is the part that makes the answer true for every existing installation: the walk is not entered unless it must be.** The gates run in this order and the order is part of the approval: `notif.showDebts`, then `debtSettled(d)`, then `debtOutstanding(d) > 0`, then the schedule's shape, and only then the walk. **On a database with no schedules — which is every installation before `SCH-02` and every user who has not typed three numbers — the additional cost is exactly zero steps.**

**Fourth, `C44` governs and is why no measurement is required.** `perf.js`'s figures may corroborate a standing deferral; they may not fire a trigger, close an item or schedule work. **Requiring a measurement before adding a loop thirty-three times smaller than the one three branches above it would be process the object does not need.** If the bell ever becomes measurably slow, the pre-ruled answer is the one-`Map` ledger fix, **not** the removal of this walk.

**What is refused by name, because it is the "obvious optimisation" that would arrive:** computing the month offset arithmetically from `firstDue` to today instead of stepping. **That is a second recurrence engine.** `stepDate`'s own comment records what two engines cost this repository — *"Two engines, one defect, twice"* — and `nextPlannedDue`'s says *"The walk stays a walk."* **`BLR11`.**

## §4(d) — when the schedule has run out

> **Confirmed, and it stops being a special case.**

Every instalment in the past means the walk yields nothing, so the slot holds `dueDate` and the item is **byte-identical to the one that ships today** — same title, same sub, same figure, same urgency, same no-lower-bound overdue behaviour. **A debt whose agreed payments have all fallen due goes on reminding on its due date exactly as it does now.**

**It falls out of the §4(a) rule rather than being handled by a branch**, which is the difference between a shape and a set of cases. The request stated it so it would be a decision; it is now a consequence, which is better.

**And the honest reading of what the request called "going quiet in the schedule sense": nothing goes quiet.** The only thing that stops being said is a thing the application was never entitled to say — §3.

## §4(e) — what the item says

> **Ruled, `title` included, and the copy is closed at these two strings permanently, on the way in.** The `.debt-totals`, `FEE-04`, settle-sheet, `SCH-02`, `PDT-01`, `EFF-02` and `ORD-02` precedents, applied before three rounds of growth rather than after.

| | Due-date item — unchanged | Agreed-payment item — ruled here |
|---|---|---|
| `key` | `debt:<id>` | `debt:<id>` — the same key, one item per debt |
| `type` | `'debt'` | `'debt'` — **binding, see `BLN1`** |
| `icon` | the existing debt icon | the same icon, unchanged |
| `title` | `Debt due: <name>` | **`Agreed payment: <name>`** |

| `date` / `daysUntil` / `urgency` | as today | the chosen date, through the **same** urgency expression |

**Why `Agreed payment` and not `Debt due` again.** The two items carry different figures — one is the balance, one is a single payment — so a user seeing the same title with a different number would reasonably read a monthly instalment as the whole amount owed. **The title is what makes the sub honest.** It names the object exactly as this module names it everywhere else — what the user **agreed to pay** — it makes no claim about conduct, and it is understandable without training, which `project.md` requires of every screen and `product-strategy.md` makes stricter.

**Why the date is the ISO date the module already prints.** No new date formatter, no localisation, no *"15 October"*. The existing debt item prints `<dueDate>` raw and `PDT-01`'s line prints an ISO date for the same reason.

**What it may never say, and this list is the copy closure rather than advice.** No count, no ordinal, no *"payment 4 of 12"*, no *"instalment N"* — **a count is the instalments-remaining figure `SCR6` refuses, wearing a label, and the request was right to say so.** No *behind*, *missed*, *arrears*, *remaining*, *on track*, *due since*, or any other word comparing the schedule with `db.debtPayments`. No outstanding figure, no agreed total, no second money figure of any kind. No countdown, no *"free by"* (`PDR5`). No congratulation, no exclamation, no encouragement. **No Mongolian string** — the language layer still needs its own scoped proposal and its own ruling, and nothing here authorises one.

## §5.7 — whether this ships at all

> **It ships, as one item, after one question to the owner. Refusal was a real answer, I held it open through the whole of this ruling, and it loses on three grounds and one sentence already in the file.**

**I took refusal seriously and here is its best case, stated fairly.** Nobody asked. The owner has not requested it. `SCD2` has been carried unchanged through three rulings without anybody reaching for it. The due-date item already exists and already goes overdue. **And work not done is the cheapest work there is** — this project's own principle, applied nine times in the last ruling alone.

**It loses, and the first ground is the file's.** The debt branch's comment states why the bell is the right surface: *"a due date is a DATED COMMITMENT, not money spent — the same thing a goal deadline is, reminded the same way, gated by its own setting so a user who does not want it can turn it off."* **An agreed instalment is that same object.** It is a dated commitment, stated by the user, stored on the record, on the same screen, gated by the same setting. **The rule admitting it is shipped, and this is it applying to a second date rather than a new rule being written.**

**Second: for a scheduled debt the bell is currently silent about the date the user must act on, for the whole life of the loan but one week.** A twelve-month contract has a `dueDate` eleven months out. The application holds the exact date of every payment in between and says nothing about any of them. **`product-strategy.md` — the owner's document — puts "Due-date reminders" on the permanently-free list, under a mission whose first item is "record what is actually owed, so the number is known rather than feared."** The gap between a stated promise and the build is not preference.

**Third: `project.md` names the unbuilt pair by name.** *"Scheduled repayments and reminders are not built… What has NOT been built — repayment schedules and reminders — is carried as a deferred item in the standing decision, with a trigger."* **Schedules shipped. This is the other half, and after it the Debts entry in `project.md` describes what exists** — which is the property that section says it is meant to hold.

**And a fourth, which is about this shape rather than the feature.** Under §4(a) the change **cannot make the bell quieter, cannot produce a second item, cannot displace an overdue prompt and cannot fire for a settled debt.** Its blast radius is one branch, one date, one label. **Refusing a change that removes a real gap and provably cannot remove a reminder would be preference wearing restraint — the phrase I used at `ODR3` and it applies here for the same reason.**

**Why the owner is asked anyway, and why this departs from `PDT-01`.** `PDT-01` did not gate because what landed was **smaller** than what the owner had been told to expect. **This is larger than anything they have been told.** They said yes to *recording* what a user agreed to pay. Every item since has been visible only to somebody already on the Debts screen or inside ✎. **This one reaches a phone's notification tray.** `product-strategy.md` says where authority sits — *"The owner of this file knows this market and the repository does not"* — and `PK5` said an architect rules shapes while a person rules product. **Whether the phone buzzes monthly is product.**

**The question, and it is the whole gate.** *"When you have agreed to pay every month, should the app remind you a few days before each payment, instead of only once on the final due date?"* **Yes, and `BEL-01` is written as ruled. No, and `BEL-01` is not written and `SCD2` stays discharged — the question was asked and answered, and it does not return as a deferral.**

## Conflict Rulings

**There is no Engineering Manager report and therefore no recorded conflict list; stated rather than left silent.** These are the nine collisions I found between the request, the standing record and the source. Each is ruled.

### BLK1 — `SCD2`'s fence *"it does not silently displace the due-date item"* against displacement

**Ruled: displacement, said explicitly, and bounded so the word means something.** The fence's two clauses admitted exactly two shapes and the request enumerated them correctly. What the request did not see is that unbounded displacement lets a schedule silence an overdue obligation — reachable because `PDR8` deliberately permits a contract whose last instalment falls after its stated due-by date. **So the slot holds the nearer date, the safety is arithmetic rather than discipline, and the fence's stated worry — one key holding two facts on different days — is disarmed by the `title`, not by the key. A key is an identity, not a claim.** `planned:<id>` has held a different occurrence on different days since before this module existed.

### BLK2 — `EFD1`'s third-reader clause against this item

**Ruled: `EFD1` fires, by its own words, and is not built inside `BEL-01`.** Its row says the trigger is an observed wrong `firstDue` *"or the same field acquiring a third reader when `SCD2` fires"*. **That is a deferral working exactly as written — the author anticipated this day and named it — and honouring it means opening a request, not bolting a refusal onto a bell commit.** Two unrelated changes are never one piece of work. **Recorded here because a fired trigger left unstated is how a fence becomes a habit.**

### BLK3 — the request's reading of `SCR6` against `PDK2`'s conduct test

**Ruled: the request's §3 concedes too much to the shape it is arguing against, and the correction is the useful half of this document.** `PDK2` admitted the payoff date because its truth depends on no conduct. **That test governs a statement. It does not govern a prompt.** An item on a notification surface asserts that there is something to do; about a payment that may already have been made, that assertion is a claim about conduct however the sentence is worded. **The request thought the overdue-instalment shape *"looks like it slips past `SCR6`"* and refused it anyway on clearability. It does not slip past, and now both grounds are on the record.**

### BLK4 — the `!d.dueDate` early return against a schedule with no due date

**Ruled: the gate becomes "no date from either source, no item", and a debt with a schedule and no due date now reminds.** `dueDate` is genuinely optional — the edit branch writes `|| null` and the add form labels it optional. **So a user can today have told the application the exact date of every payment they agreed to make and receive no reminder at all.** This is not scope growth: it is the branch's own condition stated over the two date sources the record now has instead of the one it had when the condition was written. **And it changes no existing installation's bell without the user having typed three numbers**, because the state is only reachable after `SCH-02`. **The request did not ask this and it is the state its own shape would have left broken.**

### BLK5 — `debtSettled`'s *"a debt does not become finished because time passed"* against a reader that reads the clock

**Ruled: the divergence is deliberate, it is stated in the comment, and nobody harmonises them.** `debtSettled` is pure over the record on purpose, because a **stock** must not move with the calendar. **The next agreed payment is not a stock; it is the next element of a dated series, and `nextPlannedDue` has read `todayISO()` for exactly this reason since before the Debts module.** Two readers over the same record with opposite clock contracts is the kind of thing a later reader "tidies", which is why it is a sentence in the file rather than a sentence only in this report.

### BLK6 — `DEBT_SCHEDULE_MONTHS_MAX`'s comment counts its readers

**Ruled: repaired in this commit, as a rule, naming no count.** It reads *"because it now has two readers - `debtPayoffDate` and `debtEffectiveAnnualRate`"*. **`ARCH-01` is explicit — state the rule, not the tally; never write "the only", "all", or a count unless something enforces it — and `SK6` is the worked example of what happens when nobody does.** The rule is that the ceiling has more than one reader and a second copy would be one to keep in step. **A comment a ruling falsifies is repaired in the commit that falsifies it, with the replacement landing in the same commit.**

**Ruled: answered at source and closed with no work.** `SCN5` said that if the debt-edit branch did not already refresh the badge, that was a pre-existing question and not `SCH-02`'s to open. **It becomes this item's question, because editing a schedule now changes what the bell says — and the answer is that the branch already reads `const ok = save(); renderDebts(); updateBellBadge();`.** **No new write site, the count stays where it is, and nobody adds one.** `SN6`'s finding is not re-tested and not widened.

**BLN6 — the request's §7 is right that putting the schedule beside the ledger would be one line, and that sentence is what this module should be quoted on.** *"The reason not to is not that it would be untrue. **What stays, and is not open for discussion.** Everything in the round-9 standing decision, the Round 11–17 supplementals and the twelve design-request rulings, in full, **with no off-limits entry narrowed by this document.** The single self-contained `expense-pwa\index.html` that runs by being opened from disk: no framework, no build step, no bundler, no component abstraction, **no new dependency and no new primitive — reaffirmed against this request specifically: no date library and no schedule library, because `stepDate` is the one definition of a recurrence step and a second one has cost this repository twice.** `localStorage`, one write seam, `load()` total, quarantine before write, numbered append-only migrations. **Offline-first, mobile-first, and correctness of financial data above everything** — and this request is decided by that last clause twice: once in refusing an item the record could never retract, and once in refusing a displacement rule that would let the application go quieter about an overdue obligation because it had learned more about the debt. A debt figure never reaches a period-filtered surface (`C38`). A collection that must never reach a total keeps its own array (`C39`). A figure the application computed by a model of its own is labelled as such (`C36`), and `C36` is violated by hiding a disclosure, never by hiding a figure. Comments state rules, never tallies (`ARCH-01`). A citation names the thing, not the place (`C43`). **The record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it.** **This application arranges and states; it does not recommend, project, advise or score.**

**What changes — five, and all five are readings rather than structures.**

1. **A reminder is a prompt, not a statement, and its test is not whether it is true but whether the user can make it go away.** An item nothing in the record can clear is furniture, and furniture on a notification surface teaches the user to ignore the surface — which costs the items that *are* actionable. **This is the rule that decided §3 and it is the most portable thing in this ruling.** It is the necessary complement to `PDK2`: a figure is admissible when its truth is independent of conduct; **an item is admissible when its disappearance is within the user's reach.**
2. **Where one slot can hold two dates, it holds the nearer — and learning more may never make the application say less.** `min(a, b) <= cutoff` whenever `a <= cutoff`, so the property is arithmetic and does not depend on anyone remembering it. Same class as `SAV-01`'s exclusivity, which `PDR4` established must be provable rather than observed.
3. **A key is an identity, not a claim.** One key holding two **differently labelled** facts on different days is safe; one **label** meaning two things is not. That narrows the schedule ruling's Q4 worry to what it was actually about, and it is why `planned:<id>` has always been sound.
4. **A deferral that fires is discharged, not re-tabled — third consecutive application.** `SCD2` becomes an item and leaves the table. **And a deferral whose trigger another item fires is stated as fired in the ruling that fires it** (`EFD1`), never inherited silently and never absorbed into the firing commit.
5. **The binding question for a feature on an irreversible surface is not what it may claim but what it could never take back.** §7's sentence, generalised. The schedule and the ledger are both in the record and putting them side by side is one line of code; the reason not to is that the application would have no way to stop saying it.

**Off limits this quarter — everything on the prior lists, plus five:**

- **Any bell item, OS notification or on-screen prompt about an instalment that has already fallen due**, in any form or wording, and any mechanism for clearing one — a ledger, a mark, a cursor, a module-level variable, or an inference from `db.debtPayments`.
- **More than one bell item per debt**, on any surface, for any reason.
- **A second notification setting for a sub-case of an existing one**, and any control that chooses which of two dates a single reminder reports.
- **A second recurrence engine, a second schedule ceiling, or an arithmetic month-offset shortcut** anywhere in this application.
- **Any change to what the notification sheet's actions do, or auto-recording a payment when an instalment falls due**, now that an item exists for such a fix to hang on.

**Risks I am recording, not scheduling. None is a finding and no report raised any of them.**

- **Mine, and the largest.** **This is the first time a debt produces a recurring notification, and nobody has lived with it.** A user with three scheduled debts meets a bell digit roughly three times a month and can reach the operating system monthly. **The only off switch takes the due-date reminder with it** (`BLD1`). This is the one part of the item that could make a user turn the feature off rather than use it, and the owner's gate is where that risk is priced.
- **Mine, and the sharpest.** **`firstDue` is still the one stored field in this module that nothing refuses, and after this it rings a bell.** `PDT-01`'s line shows a wrong date and does not stop one; `EFR17` refuses the only cross-check available; `EFD1` now carries it as a fired trigger rather than a waiting one. **The `min` rule softens the direction of the common error and is not a guard.**
- **Mine, from §4(a).** **A schedule can only make the bell earlier — and "earlier" includes "urgent".** A debt that was quiet for eleven months becomes urgent for three days every month. That is the feature working as ruled, and it will be read by somebody as the application nagging.
- **Mine, from `BLN4`.** The outstanding figure leaves the bell whenever the schedule item renders. A user who only ever meets that item never sees their balance on that surface.
- **Mine, from `PDD1`.** **Third consecutive ruling that accepts "nothing" as the answer to which debts carry a schedule.** The bell is not the Debts screen and this does not close it.
- **Mine, from §4(c).** **I have approved a walk nobody has measured, on the ground that a larger unmeasured walk already ships beside it.** That argument is sound and it is also the argument that would admit the next one. **The ceiling and the gate order are what bound it; if either is ever removed because "the validator already checks that", they do not.**
- **Carried and unchanged.** The instalment field is one digit away from a wrong record and the sum rule is the only guard. Monthly is baked into the schedule's shape rather than a field. "Cost so far" still overstates an early settlement. A backup restored by an older build loses `settledOn` and `schedule`. No archive on the Debts screen. `totalToRepay` has no edit trail. The four figures above the list still count debts the list has folded away and nothing says so (`ODD3`). The finished group forgets it was open (`ODD1`). The 390 guard has 94px of slack and the next true sentence above the list spends it. **Savings Goals still has no probe anywhere in `tools\harness\`, three rounds older.** `renderDebts` walks the payment ledger roughly seven times per debt and the pre-ruled one-`Map` fix stays unscheduled — **and `BEL-01` does not fire it.** Every string is a literal and the language layer still needs its own scoped proposal and its own ruling. The add form stands at ten controls and the action row at five; **this request adds to neither, deliberately.**

## Final Recommendation

**Put the one question to the owner first and take nothing until it is answered: when they have agreed to pay every month, should the app remind them a few days before each payment instead of only once on the final due date — knowing that a yes means the phone can buzz about a debt roughly monthly, and that the only off switch also turns off the due-date reminder.** On a yes, take `BEL-01` alone, in one commit: one named reader over the schedule object returning the next occurrence at or after today or `null`, shape-defensive with the failure direction "no schedule", walking `stepDate`'s monthly branch with `anchorDay` from `firstDue` under the existing `DEBT_SCHEDULE_MONTHS_MAX`; one changed branch in `computeReminders` whose gates run settled, outstanding, shape, walk, and whose item states the **nearer** of `dueDate` and the next instalment with the due date winning ties; two strings — `Agreed payment: <name>` and `<date> · <instalment> due` — closed permanently on the way in; the debt branch's comment and the ceiling constant's comment both repaired in the same commit; and one new flow in `D:\3_Claude\PowerApps\tools\harness\debts.js` whose load-bearing assertion is that **an overdue due date beside a future instalment still renders the overdue item**, with "make the schedule always win" and "drop the at-or-after-today floor" as the two perturbations that redden it. **Then stop: the card gets nothing, the tiles get nothing, the settings screen gets nothing, the payment sheet is not pre-filled, and `EFD1` — which this item fires — comes back as its own scoped request.** **The sentence I want carried out of this ruling is the one that decided §3 and §4(a) together: a reminder is not a statement but a prompt, so the test is not whether the application could truthfully say it but whether the user could ever make it stop — and by the same rule, an application that learns more about a debt may speak sooner about it and may never, on that knowledge, go quiet.**
