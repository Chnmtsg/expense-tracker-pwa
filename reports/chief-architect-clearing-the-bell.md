## The ruling in one screen

1. **"Delete" may mean one thing here and one only: an act the user could already perform from the record's own screen, reached from the bell, writing a fact the application already stores with the meaning it already has.** It may never mean a stored dismissal, a deleted record, a mute, a timeout, or an existing field written with a meaning it does not carry. **And the word "delete" and the ✕ glyph may never appear in this sheet** — there is nothing to delete, and a ✕ beside a debt reads as the cascade the Debts screen confirms with a dialog.
2. **Shape A, narrowed to one of the four types: the debt row.** One added action — **`Mark settled`** — handing off to `openDebtSettleModal`, the sheet the card's ✓ already opens. **Nothing else in the sheet changes.**
3. **Shape A's planned half is REFUSED, and it is refused on financial correctness rather than on scope.** The request proposes *Skip this one* writing `recLastDone` *"exactly as logging it would"*. It is not exactly as logging it would: logging writes the actual expense **and** the cursor. `recLastDone`'s documented meaning is *"the most recent occurrence logged as an actual expense"*, and **three shipped surfaces render it as that claim** — `renderExpenses` prints `✓ Paid` and `✓ Logged as actual on <date>`, `renderDaily` prints `✓ Paid`. A bare cursor write makes all three state that money was spent and recorded when no record exists. **`CLR5`.**
4. **Shape A's goal half is `nothing`, said out loud as the request asked.** There is no fact to write, the row already carries the act that clears it honestly, and inventing "gave up" as a stored fact is a new schema field and a speculation. **A user with a missed deadline on an unreached goal keeps that row for ever and this ruling does not fix it.** `CLD1` is the door, and it is a **product** question for the owner, not a shape question for me.
5. **Shape D is taken for three of the four types** — planned, goal, goal-recurring. That is a ruling, not a gap.
6. **The three unfloored branches are a mix, and none of them is a defect.** Debts: deliberate, the comment already says so, permanent. Planned one-off: the rule working — *"the absence of `recLastDone` … is not evidence a payment was missed"* — and the row already carries the act that clears it. Goal deadlines: a design nobody chose, weakest of the three, documented here and deferred rather than patched.
7. **An overdue row does NOT become a distinct state, and refusing that is what makes this the smallest change.** The added action renders on **every** debt row, because a debt row only exists for a live, unsettled debt and a debt is settleable whenever the user settles it — gating on overdue would hide the control in precisely the early-settlement case the whole `SET-` arc was built for.
8. **No confirm, because nothing new writes on tap.** The line is stated at §4(c).
9. **The sheet does not write to `db`, and the goal auto-contribution handler stays exactly one precedent.** Four of five handlers hand off; this makes it five of six.
10. **§4(e) confirmed as the owner answered it.** `updateBellBadge` is byte-identical. Any reduction in the badge is a side effect and is not the reason for anything.

## §1 — what "delete" may mean, ruled first because it is structural

> **Ruled: "delete" here may mean exactly one thing — a CLEARING ACT. An act the user could already perform from the record's own screen, offered from the bell, writing a fact the application already stores, with the meaning that fact already carries, through the write path that already owns it. Nothing else. `CLR1`, `CLR3`, `CLR4`, `CLR5`, `CLR7`.**

The request's table is correct and the owner's answer removes three of its four rows. What remains needs a boundary, because "mark it dealt with" is a sentence, not a shape, and four different shapes could wear it.

**The test, and it is the portable half of this ruling.** *A bell row may be cleared only by a fact that would have cleared it anyway.* The row is derived; the only way to change a derived row is to change what it derives from. So the question is never "how do we remove the row" but "**which stored fact, already meaningful, already the user's to state, does this row depend on**" — and if the answer is *none*, the honest ruling is nothing, not a new fact.

That test does four things at once, and each is a refusal the request needed:

- **It kills the stored dismissal** (Shape B, and the owner killed it too) — a dismissal is a fact about the *row*, and there is no row to hold a fact.
- **It kills deleting the record from the bell** — `✕` on the Debts screen cascades a whole payment ledger behind a dialog naming the count. **A control that destroys a ledger may not appear in a list of prompts.** `CLR3`.
- **It kills the per-record mute** — for a debt it is `settledOn` wearing a hat, which the request said and which is right; for anything else it is a new field.
- **And it kills the shape the owner's own answer selected, in one of its two halves** — because `recLastDone` written alone is a fact written with a meaning it does not have. **A field is not "existing storage" if the act writes it to mean something new.** `CLR5`, and §3 below.

**The word and the glyph are closed on the way in.** No **Delete**, no **Dismiss**, no **Clear**, no **✕**, no **×**, no swipe, no long-press, no hidden gesture anywhere in `#notifBody`. **The label names the act** — the sheet's four existing labels all do (`✓ Log ₮50,000`, `Custom amount`, `+ Payment`, `+ Add to goal`), and this is the property that makes the sheet safe to tap on a phone. `CLR7`.

## §2 — the three unfloored branches: defect, design, or mix

> **Ruled: a MIX, and zero of the three is a defect. Two are designs already decided and one is a design nobody decided. Nothing is floored by this item. `CLR2`.**

**Debts — a design, documented, permanent.** The comment states it: *"NO LOWER BOUND IS ADDED. An overdue due date reminds exactly as it did."* **An obligation that is still owed may never age out of this application, behind any setting, after any number of days.** `C1`'s direction and `SET-01`'s own justification read backwards: a debt wrongly reported finished is the application under-reporting what its record holds. **Ruled permanently. This is the clause of Shape C that is not merely unwanted but forbidden.** `CLR2`.

**Planned, one-off — a design, documented, and the request's framing of it is the wrong way round.** `nextPlannedDue`'s comment already carries the rule: *"The absence of `recLastDone` means the user has never LOGGED an occurrence of this series. It is not evidence a payment was missed… The app records bookkeeping actions and may not assert a missed payment from the absence of one."* A one-off plan sitting in the bell is not the application accusing the user; it is a commitment the user made to themselves that the application has no record of being met. **And the sheet already offers the act that clears it.** It is not a floor problem. Not a defect.

**Goal deadlines — a design nobody decided, and it is the weakest thing in the bell.** There is no comment, no ruling and no recorded intent. A missed deadline on an unreached goal reminds for ever, it is the only one of the three with **no** clearing act reachable from the bell, and — unlike a debt — **nobody is owed.** `product-strategy.md`'s user is under financial stress; a permanent, unclearable row that says they did not save enough by a date they chose is the `PR2` harm the bell ruling refused at §3, arriving through a branch nobody looked at. **I am not fixing it here**, because the only storage-free fix is a floor and a floor is a product decision the owner has not been asked about, and the only other fix is a new stored fact. **`CLD1`, with a trigger, and it is an owner question rather than an architect question.**

**And no comment is added to the goal or planned branches in this commit.** A comment in a branch this item does not otherwise touch is scope growth, and two unrelated changes are never one piece of work. **Pre-ruled: if either branch is ever edited for another reason, the no-floor design lands as a rule in that commit** (`ARCH-01` — a rule, never a tally, citing functions rather than coordinates). Until then the record is this ruling and `HANDOFF.md`.

## §3 — Shape A as narrowed, and D taken for three of four

> **Ruled: A for the debt row only. D for planned, goal and goal-recurring — and D is taken as a real answer, not as a residue. `CLB-01`.**

**Why A wins on the debt row, and this is the one place the list is not merely long but wrong-and-uncorrectable.** `SET-01` exists because **a debt repaid early is finished for less than the agreed total, and no arithmetic over this record can tell that apart from somebody who still owes the difference.** A user in that state — the debt actually over, the record still showing an outstanding balance — has a permanently urgent bell row about money they do not owe, and the fact that corrects it is a fact **they already own and the application already stores**. It is reachable from the card's ✓ and from nowhere else. **So the bell holds a statement the application knows how to be wrong about and offers no correction for at the point where the user meets it.** That is a real risk removed by a real act, and it is the only one of the four branches where that sentence is true.

**Why the planned row gets nothing, and it is the finding this ruling turns on.** *Skip this one* writing `recLastDone` is not "the existing field, exactly as logging it would". `logPlannedAsActual` writes **the expense and then the cursor**; the cursor's documented meaning is *"the most recent occurrence logged as an actual expense"*; and `renderExpenses` renders `✓ Paid` and `✓ Logged as actual on <date>` off it while `renderDaily` renders `✓ Paid`. **A skipped plan would print "Logged as actual on 2026-08-12" beside no actual.** `WORK-08`'s rule and `SET-02`'s — *one claim, one meaning, at every site that renders it* — forbid it directly. The only way to admit the act is to broaden the field's meaning and reword three surfaces to something that covers both, which **destroys the application's ability to distinguish a plan that was paid from a plan that was abandoned**, permanently, in the record. **That is a worse outcome than a row the user can already clear with the button beside it.** `CLR5`.

**And the honest gap is named rather than filled.** A one-off plan the user decided *not* to incur has no clearing act anywhere in this application that does not either fabricate an expense or delete the budget line and its past-period figures. **That is real and this ruling does not fix it**, because every fix available today is a new stored fact or a false one. It is not deferred with a trigger, because no observation would change the arithmetic above; it is recorded as a standing limitation in the Architecture Strategy's risk list.

**Why the goal row gets nothing, said out loud as the request asked.** Four reasons, in order of weight: **(1)** there is no fact — a goal has no `settledOn`, no cursor and no finished state other than `saved >= g.target`, which `computeReminders` already tests; **(2)** the row already carries `+ Add to goal`, which clears it honestly the moment the target is reached; **(3)** the other two honest clearing acts — move the deadline, delete the goal — live in the goal editor, and pulling an *editor* into a list of prompts is a far larger surface than any part of this request; **(4)** a "gave up on this goal" fact is a new schema field invented to remove a row, which is the premature generalisation this role exists to refuse. **`CLR11`.**

**Why goal-recurring gets nothing.** It is floored by `computeNextRecurring` and already carries two actions. It is not a source of the owner's complaint and nothing is proposed for it.

**And D is a real answer for those three.** Every remaining row is true, every remaining row has an action, and the application is not entitled to make any of them go away on the user's behalf.

## §4(b) — does an overdue row become a distinct state?

> **Ruled: NO. The added action renders on every debt row, overdue or not. Overdue remains what it is today — a label in the `title` line and a CSS class that was already there. No new state, no new predicate, no new group, no re-ordering. `CLR8`.**

**This is the place where the obvious design is the bigger one.** The request is right that every shape it proposed treats overdue as distinct for the first time. **The one I am approving does not, and refusing to is what makes it the smallest change.**

Three grounds. **First and decisive: a debt row only exists for a debt that is live** — `computeReminders`' debt branch returns early on `debtSettled(d)` and on `debtOutstanding(d) <= 0` — **so every debt row in this sheet is a debt the user could truthfully mark settled.** Gating the control on `daysUntil < 0` would hide it in exactly the case the whole `SET-` arc was built for: **the user who settles early, before the due date, for less than the agreed total.** A control that disappears in its own headline scenario is not a narrowing; it is a defect.

**Second: it is less code and less state.** The action column already switches on `r.type`. Adding a constant second button to one branch is a string. Adding a *conditional* second button is a string plus a predicate plus a rule about when overdue begins, and `daysUntil <= 0` versus `< 0` is precisely the kind of boundary this file has got wrong before.

**Third: a sheet whose controls move around is a sheet users stop trusting.** The same row showing one button on Tuesday and two on Wednesday teaches the user that the bell's controls are conditional, which is the property that makes people stop reading a surface.

**What is refused with it, because these are the shapes that arrive as "while we're in here":** an `.overdue` class or style; a separate **Overdue** group or heading; a count of overdue rows anywhere; a re-ordering that sinks or floats overdue rows — **the sort stays `a.date.localeCompare(b.date)`**, which is the honest order for a list of dated commitments and which `ORD-01` establishes is a presentation default belonging to the owner, who has not asked. `CLR8`.

## §4(c) — does a clearing act confirm, and where is the line?

> **Ruled: NO CONFIRM, and no dialog of any kind — because nothing this item adds writes on tap. The line is stated below and it is general. `CLR12`.**

**The line, and I am stating it as a rule rather than answering this one case.**

> **A confirmation is owed when an act writes on tap AND cannot be undone by reopening the sheet that performed it.**
>
> **A hand-off is not an act.** Opening a sheet that asks for a value and carries a Save button is already the confirmation, and a dialog in front of it is a second confirmation of a decision the user has not yet made.

Run the three shipped cases through it and it reproduces all three without adjustment. **The Debts screen's `✕` confirms** — it writes on tap, it cascades a payment ledger, nothing reopens. **`logPlannedAsActual` from `✓ Log` does not** — it writes on tap, and it is the sheet's one genuinely one-tap act. **`openDebtSettleModal` does not, and its own ruling says why** — *"No confirm dialogue either way: the delete carries one because it cascades and cannot be undone, and this is undone by reopening the sheet."*

**So the approved act inherits the settle sheet's answer rather than needing one.** It writes nothing; it opens a sheet with one date field, a helper sentence and a Save button; the save branch refuses a settlement before the borrow date; and the reversal is emptying the same field in the same sheet. **`SET-03` already ruled this and the bell is not a reason to re-rule it.**

**Refused with it:** a `confirmDialog()` in front of the hand-off; a toast on the hand-off; a second helper sentence in the settle sheet explaining that it was reached from the bell — **the settle sheet's helper is closed at one string permanently and it is already the sentence that explains the bell**: *"Marking it finished stops this app reporting it as outstanding and stops it reminding you about it."* `CLN4`. And **no defaulting, stamping or pre-filling of the settlement date from the bell** — the field arrives at `todayISO()` as a convenience the user overwrites, exactly as it does from the card, and the bell has no business changing that. `CLR12`.

## §4(d) — may the sheet write to `db`?

> **Ruled: NO. The notification sheet HANDS OFF; it does not write. The goal auto-contribution handler is grandfathered, stays exactly one precedent, and may not be cited as licence for a second. `CLR6`.**

**The request found the precedent and counted it correctly. Exactly one is the wrong number to generalise from, and it is the wrong one to generalise from.**

**What the sheet's four other handlers do** is close the sheet and open the modal that owns the write — `data-edit-planned` → `openLogPlannedModal`, `data-debt-reminder-pay` → `openDebtPaymentModal`, `data-goal-add` → `openContributeModal` — with a stated reason: *"two stacked sheets on a phone leave the Escape/back key ambiguous."* **That is the rule. `data-log-goalrec` is the exception, and `data-convert-planned` is the near-miss** — it does not write inline either; it calls `logPlannedAsActual`, the single documented writer, which exists *because* the same write was nearly done twice.

**Why the exception may not spread.** A write from this sheet needs its own `save()` outcome handling, its own failed-save contract (*"a sheet that re-rendered after a FAILED save would show the reminder gone… while the toast beside it said the write was rejected"*), its own `refocusModalTop` site, its own re-render and its own cross-screen render call. **`refocusModalTop`'s comment names four sites and says the app-wide focus sweep stays off limits.** Every inline write in this sheet is a fifth copy of that contract. **The hand-off has none of it, because the modal it opens already owns all of it.**

**And this refusal kills the planned half of Shape A a second time, independently of §3** — *Skip this one* has no modal to hand off to, so it could only be an inline write. Two independent refusals of the same shape is worth recording: it means the shape is not merely unaffordable here, it is unshaped.

## An Owner Gate — none is owed, and here is the test

## Rulings on What the Request Did Not Ask

Six. None is a finding; no reviewer raised any of them, and I record them as ruled scope and as risks.

**CLN1 — the heaviest act in this sheet is the one nobody questioned, and it is the planned row's.** `✓ Log <amount>` writes an actual expense **on tap**, with no sheet, no confirmation and no date the user chose, and **deleting that expense afterwards does not reset `recLastDone`** — so the plan stays marked `✓ Paid` over a spend that has been removed. **That is the least reversible control in the notification sheet and it ships today.** I am not touching it: it is out of this request's scope, it was ruled into existence deliberately (the one-tap path exists so the common case costs one tap), and fixing it is a change to the planned module's cursor semantics with its own harms. **Recorded as a risk, not a finding, and it is the sharpest thing I found while reading for this request.** It also inverts the request's instinct: the asymmetry between the types runs the opposite way from the one §3 assumed.

Refused at `CLR8` and recorded here so the next reader knows it was considered.**

**CLN3 — settling from the bell calls `renderDebts()` while the user may be on Home, and that is safe.** The `editCtx.kind === 'debtSettle'` branch runs `const ok = save(); renderDebts(); updateBellBadge();` unconditionally. `renderDebts` writes only inside `#debts` — **`WORK-176`'s containment assertion is the guard and it is capable of failing since `WORK-191`** — so rendering an off-screen list is a no-op the user never sees. **No screen-awareness is added, no navigation happens, and the user stays where they were.** Verified at source rather than assumed.

**CLN4 — the settle sheet already carries the sentence that explains the bell, and it gains no word.** *"Marking it finished stops this app reporting it as outstanding and stops it reminding you about it."* **It was written before a bell control existed and it is exactly right now that one does.** The helper is closed at one string permanently.

## Architecture Strategy — next quarter

**What stays, and is not open for discussion.** Everything in the round-9 standing decision, the Round 11–17 supplementals and the thirteen design-request rulings, in full, **with exactly one off-limits entry narrowed by this document (`CLK1`) and nothing else moved.** The single self-contained `expense-pwa\index.html` that runs by being opened from disk: no framework, no build step, no bundler, no component abstraction, no new dependency and no new primitive. `localStorage`, one write seam, `load()` total, quarantine before write, numbered append-only migrations. **Offline-first, mobile-first, and correctness of financial data above everything** — and this request is decided by that last clause outright: the shape the owner's own answer selected was refused in one of its halves because it would have made three surfaces state a spend that never happened. A debt figure never reaches a period-filtered surface (`C38`). A collection that must never reach a total keeps its own array (`C39`). A modelled figure is labelled as such (`C36`), and `C36` is violated by hiding a disclosure, never by hiding a figure. Comments state rules, never tallies (`ARCH-01`). A citation names the thing, not the place (`C43`). The record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it. **This application arranges and states; it does not recommend, project, advise or score.** **And from the last ruling, reaffirmed and used again here: a reminder is a prompt, not a statement, and its test is whether the user can make it go away.**

**What changes — five, and all five are readings rather than structures.**

1. **A derived row is cleared only by a fact that would have cleared it anyway.** The row is not a thing; it is a consequence. So the question is never *how do we remove it* but **which stored fact, already meaningful and already the user's to state, does it depend on** — and when the answer is *none*, the ruling is **nothing**, not a new fact. This is the complement to the last ruling's admissibility test: an item is admissible when its disappearance is within the user's reach, **and the mechanism of that disappearance must already be a fact the record holds.**
2. **"The field already exists" is never sufficient. The test is whether the act writes it with the meaning it already carries.** `recLastDone` exists and `settledOn` exists; one of the two proposed acts was free and one would have changed what a field means at three render sites. **Re-using storage and re-using meaning are different things, and only the second is free.** `CLK3`.
3. **A notification surface hands off; it does not write.** Every inline write on such a surface needs its own failed-save contract, its own re-render, its own focus restoration and its own cross-screen render call — all of which the modal it would hand off to already owns. **One existing exception is grandfathered and bounded at one; an exception is what a rule is stated against, not a pattern to extend.**
4. **A control that exists for an early or exceptional case may not be gated on the ordinary one.** Gating `Mark settled` on *overdue* would have hidden it in the early-settlement case the whole act was built for. **Where a gate is proposed for tidiness, check first whether it excludes the scenario the feature exists for.**
5. **A gate is owed when a change alters what the application does to a user who is not looking at it, or asks the owner to choose between two products; a sentence is owed when what lands is smaller than what they asked for.** `BEL-01` gated because it reached a notification tray. This one adds a labelled control to a sheet the user opened on purpose, so it does not. **Stating the test is what stops "ask the owner" becoming either a habit or a lapse.**

**Off limits this quarter — everything on the prior lists, plus five:**

- **Any stored notification state of any kind** — a dismissal, a read/unread mark, a snooze, a per-item flag, a per-record mute, or a module-level variable standing in for one. **The bell is derived, entirely, on every call.**
- **A lower bound, floor, timeout or auto-expiry on the debt branch of `computeReminders`**, behind any setting, at any number of days. An obligation that is still owed may never stop being mentioned because time passed.
- **Any write of `recLastDone` not accompanied by the actual expense it names**, and any rewording of `✓ Paid`, `✓ Logged as actual on <date>` or `✓ Paid thru <date>` to accommodate one.
- **The word delete, dismiss, clear, hide, remove, snooze or ignore, and the `✕` glyph, anywhere inside `#notifBody`**; and any swipe, long-press or unlabelled gesture on a notification row.
- **Any write to `db` from the notification sheet's handlers**, beyond the one grandfathered goal auto-contribution handler, which is not to be extended, copied or cited as precedent.

**Risks I am recording, not scheduling. None is a finding and no report raised any of them.**

Until then it stands.**
