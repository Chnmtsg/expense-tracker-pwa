## §3 — The second-disclosure entry, and `C36`'s application to the proposed shape

**Refused. The entry stands at full width. `ABR1` and `ABR2`.**

The request makes the best argument available for the shape and it is a real argument: if the figure and its disclosure sit behind the **same** control, there is no state in which the number is legible and its qualification is not, so co-visibility is satisfied rather than evaded. Had the premise held, I would have narrowed the entry. **The premise does not hold on this screen, and it fails at source rather than on taste.**

**The ruling, and it is the whole of §3.** `renderDebts`' summary-block comment states the one-site rule in its own words: *"One site, not two: showCost is the aggregate across all debts, so it is true whenever any per-card 'Cost so far' chip is on screen. Captioning the chip as well would put the same sentence up once per debt."* `showCost` is computed from `totalInterest` across every debt. The sentence is therefore **not** the disclosure for the tile. It is the disclosure for the tile **and for every "Cost so far" chip in the list below it** — four of the five cards in `shot-debts-390-after.png` carry one, with a tugrik figure in `--danger-text`.

So the proposed shape does not move a pair. It moves one of five figures behind a control and takes the qualification for the other four with it. In the state the shape produces, the user reads *"Cost so far ₮90,000"*, *"Cost so far ₮46,154"*, *"Cost so far ₮692,308"* and *"Cost so far ₮120,000"* — four unqualified statements of this application's own even-allocation arithmetic, presented as facts about four contracts it has never seen — and the sentence that admits the app is guessing requires a gesture. That is the exact failure `C36` names, arriving by a new route.

**`C36`'s application to the shape, stated so it can be quoted, and it is a necessary condition and never a sufficient one.** *A `C36` disclosure may travel behind a control only if **every** figure it qualifies travels behind the same control, in every reachable state.* A one-site disclosure — one sentence standing for a figure that renders once per record — can never satisfy that test while the records are on screen. **And satisfying it does not license the move**: the second-disclosure entry is a separate rule with a separate reason, and on the Debts screen it independently refuses it.

**The narrowing is refused on its own terms as well, and I want all three reasons on the record, because the first one alone would leave the entry looking arbitrary.**

- **It would be a rule written for no case.** After the paragraph above there is no shape on this screen that the narrowing would license. A rule that licenses nothing is premature generalisation, and it would be quoted later by somebody who has the clause and not the ruling — which has already happened once in this project, and is why the disclaimer-binds-the-proposal clarification exists.
- **My own counter-argument stands and the request quotes it accurately.** Two chevrons on one screen is two chevrons. The first one was ruled in round 16 to move a form the user completes once out of the way of the thing they return for. A second one teaches the user that this screen hides things, which is the opposite of what the first one was for.
- **The product reason, which outranks both.** `product-strategy.md` states the mission in three ordered items, and item 2 is *"Show what the borrowing costs, in tugrik and as a rate."* "Cost so far" on the aggregate card **is** item 2. A shape whose effect is that the user must tap a chevron to see what their borrowing has cost is the application hiding its own reason for existing to save 68px. No measurement makes that trade good.

**Corroboration, and it is not the reason — it is what tells me the reason was already agreed.** The flow committed yesterday, *"the summary block states one sentence, or two when there is a cost"*, ends with an explicit co-visibility assertion (`TS_disclosure_gated`) that reddens on `details .helper`, `[hidden]` and `display:none`, under a comment saying *"a qualification the user must perform an action to read is not a qualification while the number it corrects is on screen."* Building §3's shape would require **editing that assertion to admit the shape**. That is changing the expectation instead of the application — the failure mode `C37` and `C40` exist to stop — and it is refused by name in `ABR7`.

## §2(b) — the guarantee's scope, in full

The request asks the right question and states the constraint honestly: at 320 the deficit is 174px and everything it could propose to remove above the list totals 126px, so either the guarantee is a 390 statement or it needs the chip row back, which I closed unfixed. **It is a 390 statement. I am not reopening the chip row, and the request does not ask me to.**

**Three things on the record with the ruling.**

**First, 126 is itself a 390 figure quoted against a 320 deficit.** At 320 that prose wraps to more lines, so the removable total there is larger than 126 and the deficit is also driven by a 363px card. It does not change the answer — sentence 3 cannot go at any width (`C36`), sentence 1 is the sentence that explains "Still owed" exceeding "Borrowed in total" and nobody has proposed removing it, and what remains reaches nowhere near 174 — but the request is one width-substitution away from the mistake it opens by diagnosing, and I would rather say so than let the arithmetic stand unlabelled.

**Second, the guarantee is not a claim about a device, and the flow must say so.** The frame is 820px tall and the owner's phone is not. At 390 the measured slack is −18 in an 820 frame; the same layout in a taller content area is a different number. A green reading at 390 means *one whole card fits in the fixture*, and whoever quotes it says that or says nothing.

**Third, what 320 gets instead of a guarantee, so that scoping does not read as abandoning it.** Every figure the flow gathers at 320 and 360 is recorded and labelled diagnostic, per `C42`, and that is the whole of what those widths get from this ruling. **The route back for 320 is not another 30px above the list — it is the list itself**, which is Round 17's recorded risk verbatim: every density saving here is a constant on a linear problem, and the answer when it arrives is grouping or collapse. That is not scheduled, it is not a finding, and no reviewer raised it as one.

## Conflict Rulings — collisions

### ABK1 — `.sr-only` against `.card h3`

`.card h3` sets `margin: 0 0 var(--s3) 0` at higher specificity than `.sr-only`'s `margin: -1px`, so the margin declaration survives the class. **Ruled harmless, and verified by measurement rather than by argument:** the element leaves the flow on `position: absolute`, so a surviving bottom margin contributes no height, and `width`, `height`, `overflow` and `clip-path` are uncontested. **The proof obligation sits with the commit** — the flow's re-run must show the first card's top offset falling by the heading's whole former cost. **No `!important`, no new class, no edit to `.card h3`.** If the offset does not move, the change comes back as a scoped request.

### ABK2 — `ABL-02` against the `.debt-total-value` comment and against `.kpi .value`

The comment opens with a declaration — *"--t-h2 and --w-bold, not 20px/800"* — that `ABL-02` makes false, and it also records that this block reimplemented `.kpi`'s tile. **Ruled:** the comment states a rule that survives the change (no sixth headline size on a five-step scale) wrapped around a declaration that does not, and it is repaired in the same commit that falsifies it — `ARCH-01` applied at the moment it bites rather than a round later, for the third time in this project. `.kpi .value` stays at `--t-h2`; the Dashboard is not touched; the divergence is deliberate, per-module, and the repaired comment is what tells the next reader so. **This is round-16 `WORK-07`'s pattern on a third pair of modules, and it needs no new rule.**

### ABK3 — `ABL-02` against round-16 `WORK-10`'s deferral

The request flags this honestly rather than pretending it does not touch a deferral, and it is right to. **Ruled at `ABD1`:** a deferral with a measured trigger is discharged by the measurement, never by an argument that the measurement is now unnecessary. The diagnostic already exists; reading it costs nothing.

### ABK4 — §3 against the `WORK-11` flow committed yesterday

Building §3's shape requires relaxing `TS_disclosure_gated`. **Ruled:** the assertion stands and the shape falls. Corroboration only — §3 was already refused on `C36` and on the mission — but it is the cleanest demonstration available that the reading was settled before the shape arrived and not in response to it.

### ABK5 — §4(b) against the same flow's two text assertions

Any merge must keep both protected substrings verbatim or the flow reddens. **Ruled:** the substrings are the floor and `ABR7` makes editing them out of bounds. This collision is also what proved `ABR4`: once both facts must survive verbatim, the only thing a merge can remove is connective tissue, and connective tissue is not a line.

### ABK6 — this ruling against my own Round 17 Architecture Strategy

Round 17 states, unscoped: *"The Debts screen acquires its first geometry guard, and it guards a product statement rather than a number: at least one complete debt card is reachable without scrolling."* **That sentence is read as scoped from today** — 390, in the 820px fixture — and `ABL-03`'s flow header is where the scope lives. I am correcting my own standing statement rather than letting the flow quietly under-deliver against it, which is the same discipline Round 17 applied to the `.goal-card` comment. **Nothing else in Round 17 is reopened**, and in particular the four off-limits additions all stand, including the one §3 asked me to narrow.

### ABK7 — the owner's "both, equally" against what this ruling actually delivers

The prose half gets nothing: `ABR4` refuses the merge and §3 refuses the disclosure, so the five lines of grey text above the list are **unchanged**. The tile half gets a heading removed from view and four figures one step smaller. **Said out loud, because the request's own standard is that a complaint closes explicitly or not at all: half of "both, equally" closes unfixed this round, and it is the half the owner's answer made bigger.** It is not dropped — `ABD2` and `ABD3` carry the two doors back, each with a trigger, and neither is a schedule wearing a trigger.
