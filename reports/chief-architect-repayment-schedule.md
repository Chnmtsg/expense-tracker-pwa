## Rulings on the Six Questions in §5

### Q1 (§3) — stated by the user, or is a derived schedule admissible?

**Stated. A derived schedule is inadmissible, permanently, and I am saying it as plainly as I was asked to. The proposal's load-bearing claim holds, and it holds on four grounds, of which the second is mine and is the one that decides it.**

**First, it is the prohibition by name.** `totalToRepay ÷ months` is **an assumed repayment pattern** — the exact phrase on the off-limits list at the fee narrowing, at `ER1`, at `FR3` and at `PR2`. The proposal is right that this is the object refused at the fee ruling, at early settlement and at the payoff plan three hours ago. Nothing about a fourth outing changes it.

**Second, and it is the ground the proposal did not have: a derived schedule would fire a trigger with data that predates the trigger.** `PD1` reads: *"the day this application records a repayment schedule… no market fact and no owner statement about lenders can fire this — only a change in what the application records."* A schedule computed from `principal`, `totalToRepay`, `date` and `dueDate` **records nothing**. Every one of those fields has been in the store since the decoder. So if a derived schedule were admissible, `PD1` fired before it was written, the effective rate fired six rounds ago, and two deferrals that have held this module's line since before the decoder were never fences at all — they were arithmetic waiting for somebody to perform it. **A deferral whose trigger is a new recorded fact is never fired by a derivation over facts already held.** That goes into the Architecture Strategy as a general rule, because it is the thing that will be argued next.

**Third, the arithmetic is not even available on the terms the proposal imagines.** `feeTermMonths` is a form-time function that stores nothing and is not called from any stored-record derivation; using it here is the **third term derivation** refused at `ER7`, in a module whose two existing ones carry a comment warning the next reader against unifying them. And `totalToRepay` is not a quantity that divides evenly by construction: it may carry an origination fee, an admin charge or front-loaded interest, and `debtInterestPaid`'s own comment records that **charging interest first is what the lender actually does.** An instalment derived from it is wrong in a direction the record cannot see.

**Fourth, the proposal's own reason, accepted.** A derived schedule carries exactly the uncertainty that made the projection inadmissible, so it would unlock nothing. Correct, and now the weakest of the four rather than the whole case.

**And the corollary, which is where this rule will actually be tested.** It is not enough that the user types the numbers; **the application must not supply them.** A "suggested" schedule that pre-fills the three fields from the record for the user to accept is inference wearing the calculator's clothes, and it does not survive the fee narrowing, which is five clauses: what was permitted is *a rate **the user states**, consumed by a form-time calculator that fills an existing field, **stores nothing**, shows its working.* Here there would be no user-stated input at all, and the output is **stored**. **Two clauses failed, and one is the storage clause.** `SCR3`.

### Q2 (§5.2) — the object

**Three facts, exactly as §2 states them, as ONE optional composite field on the debt record. Not fewer, not more, not a collection, not three siblings.**

> `schedule: { instalment, count, firstDue }` — absent on every existing record; absent and `null` identical; refused by the validator only when present and malformed; no migration, because there is nothing to transform.

**Why three and not fewer.** `count` is not derivable: deriving it from `firstDue` and `dueDate` is the third term derivation (`ER7`), and deriving it from `totalToRepay ÷ instalment` is Q1's prohibition with one field moved. `firstDue` is not `dueDate` — the card's own ask line calls `dueDate` *"the date this has to be repaid by"*, which is the last date, not the first. `instalment` is the fact the whole object exists for. **Remove any one and the remaining two are not a schedule.**

**Why three and not more.** **No frequency field and no period selector.** Monthly is the market fact the owner has already stated twice — a monthly rate, simple interest, whole months — and a frequency selector is `FR7`'s shape exactly: *one period, named in words, or none*, because a mis-tap on a period is a multiplicative error in a stored record. The period is named **in words on screen** and carried in the object by not existing. **No `endDate`** — it is `firstDue` stepped `count − 1` times and deriving it is free. **No amount-paid-to-date, no cursor, no status.**

**Why one composite field and not three siblings.** The three are meaningless apart. Three sibling scalars make eight presence combinations of which seven are invalid, every future reader has to test all three, and the edit branch gains three assignments and three chances to forget one. **One value cannot represent the invalid combinations at all.** The application already has the flat counter-example: a goal's `recFrequency` / `recStartDate` / `recLastLogged` / `recIntervalDays` are four siblings that `computeReminders` **re-assembles into an object** (`goalRecObj`) before it can use them. That is the flat shape paying for itself at the read site, in this file, today.

**The name is `schedule` and it is ruled, not left open.** It names the term of the contract. It is **not** a recurrence record and must never grow one: no `recFrequency`, no `lastDone`, no `recEndDate`, no anchor cursor. What may **never** be used is a status name — `schedulePaid`, `instalmentsDone`, `onTrack` — for the reason `SR1` closed: a date says what happened, a flag says what the record is.

**The validator clause, ruled here rather than left to implementation.** In `dueDate`'s and `settledOn`'s exact shape and voice: present-and-`null` is normal; present-and-malformed returns **one** message for the field; the object must be a plain object, `instalment` a finite number above zero, `count` an integer of at least one, `firstDue` a string matching `ISO_DATE_RE`. **It is NOT cross-checked against `date`, `dueDate` or `totalToRepay` in `debtProblem`**, on the reasoning already written twice in that function: an importer rejecting a whole backup over it would destroy more than it protects, and nothing derived divides by the gap. **Sense is refused at the boundary the user is standing at. Shape is refused at the border.** That two-door split is this module's own and it is not being invented here.

### Q3 (§4(a)) — `instalment × count` against `totalToRepay`

**None of the three. The answer is a fourth shape and it is smaller than all of them: refuse what cannot be this contract, accept the rounding silently, and say nothing on any surface.**

**Refuse, at the boundary, when the instalments cannot be this debt.** The write path refuses a schedule whose instalments miss the agreed total by **a whole instalment or more** — `|instalment × count − totalToRepay| < instalment`. That is not a tolerance invented to be lenient; it is **the definition of the object being stored**: N equal instalments covering an agreed total, with the remainder inside one of them. ₮113,333 × 12 against ₮1,360,000 passes with ₮4 to spare. ₮1,133,333 × 12 — the extra digit, which is the `FN3` class of error on a money field — is refused. A ₮100,000 × 12 interest-only schedule against a ₮1,360,000 total is refused **and refusing it is correct**: that contract is not N equal instalments, this object cannot describe it, and storing three numbers that omit ₮160,000 would be the record holding something that is not true. **Refused and never clamped, never rounded for the user, naming what blocks it** — the module's idiom at four existing boundaries.

**Accept the rounding silently in the record.** No second stored figure, no reconciliation field, no adjustment of the last instalment by the application. **The application does not restate what the user typed about their own money**, which is written in `debtAdd`'s own comment.

**And say nothing, anywhere.** This is where I depart from the proposal's lean, and the reason is structural rather than editorial. *Accept and say so* presumes the two figures will meet on a screen. **They never do.** Nothing in this request sums the instalments, so the product `instalment × count` is computed at exactly one moment — the boundary check — and exists nowhere else. **A sentence reconciling two figures that are never printed side by side would be the first thing in this module to create the contradiction it explains.** And there is no room for it: the card foot renders **at most one gated helper line in every reachable state**, by construction, and a fourth line spends that invariant on a problem the user does not have. **The refusal message is the only sentence, and it appears only when there is something to say** — it states the product and the agreed total, two facts, and offers no correction and no advice.

### Q4 (§4(c)) — reminders

**Nothing. `computeReminders` is untouched by this request, byte-identical, still keyed on `dueDate`, still at most one item per debt. The bell goes on counting debts because nothing has asked it to count anything else.**

Three grounds.

**First, it is a separate change with separate harms.** The bell is an OS notification. The one defect Round 16 found that a user meets in normal use lived there, and `SET-03` had to add a sixth write site to keep it honest. **A storage request does not get to change what a notification means as a side effect.** Never approve two unrelated changes as one piece of work.

**Second, the question is genuinely open and the proposal did not ask it.** A schedule has no cursor and **must not grow one** — `db.debtPayments` is ad hoc, there is no per-instalment ledger, and nothing in the record can say which instalments were paid. So what does the bell say about an instalment that fell due and was not paid? Walking to the first date at or after today reports the **next** one and silently passes over the missed one; reporting the missed one is the application asserting that it was missed, which is the performance-scoring this ruling fences out. **That is a real product question with a real answer, and it is worth its own short request rather than a paragraph of mine.**

**Third, the fear itself is unfounded, and the file already says so.** `nextPlannedDue` returns **one** date, `computeReminders` pushes **one** item per series, keyed `planned:<id>`. A user with three planned monthly series sees three items, not thirty-six. `SCK5`.

**Pre-ruled as fences, binding on `SCD2` whenever it arrives, so it cannot be decided by whoever opens the block:** at most **one** item per debt, keyed `debt:<id>` as today, and never one per instalment; **the next occurrence only**; the walk goes through **`stepDate`** and never a second `setMonth` — `stepDate`'s own comment records what that cost twice; **no stored cursor of any kind on a debt record**; gated on `debtSettled(d)` before anything else; and **it does not silently displace the due-date item** — one key holding two different facts on different days is how a user stops trusting the bell.

### Q5 (§4(d) / §5.5) — an expectation beside a fact

**Admissible, the request does not end, and the premise is false at source. This is the correction in this document.**

**What is stored is not an expectation about conduct. It is a term of an agreement already made** — the same class of object as `totalToRepay`, which is what the user agreed to pay, and `dueDate`, which is **when** they agreed to pay it. Both are statements about payments that have not happened, both have been in the record for rounds, and **`debtOutstanding` has been the subtraction between the agreed and the actual since the module shipped.** The card prints it: *"₮1,120,000 paid of ₮1,360,000."* **`dueDate` is a repayment schedule with a count of one, and this request is that fact generalised.** The module is not acquiring a new kind of object; it is acquiring a second number on one it already holds.

**And the application as a whole already holds a plan beside a record of departure from it, deliberately, with a screen for it** — `db.planned` against `db.actual`, compared under the heading *Planned vs Actual*, with `plannedOccurrencePaid`'s comment stating exactly how the cursor over-reports and why the wording says *"paid through"* rather than claiming each occurrence was ticked. So the shape is neither new to the file nor unsolved in it. **It is fenced out of the Debts module for a different reason — no debt record reaches a period-filtered surface — and that fence is reaffirmed at Q6.**

**The line I am drawing, and it is the whole of the safety here: the record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it.** No *"behind by"*, no *"missed"*, no arrears, no count of instalments paid or unpaid, no per-instalment ledger, no cursor, no colour, no chip, no badge, no figure whatsoever that compares `schedule` with `db.debtPayments`. **The application states what was agreed and what was paid, and the user does the comparing** — which is precisely what the card does today with two figures and no verdict. The two will disagree constantly, exactly as the proposal says, **and no surface in this application will ever be told.**

### Q6 (§4(b) and §4(e)) — confirmations

**§4(b) — it does not write `db.planned`. Confirmed, permanently, and the reason is already in the shipped file.** `computeReminders`' debt branch carries it: planned expenses are period-filtered and feed the Dashboard — Planned vs Actual, the Planned total, *"Left After Plan"* — while this module's binding condition is that **no debt figure reaches a Dashboard total or moves with a date filter**, with two harness flows guarding it. A schedule written into `db.planned` pushes debt money through the one door the module was built to keep shut, invisibly; and it **double-counts** the moment the user does what the payment sheet tells them to and records the repayment as an expense. **Also confirmed: no new collection.** `db.debtSchedules` is `C39`'s shape arriving from the other end and is refused before it is proposed. The schedule is a field on a record inside `db.debts` and it travels with its parent, including through the delete cascade. **The proposal was right that somebody will propose this; it is now on the off-limits list by name at `SCR7` and `SCR8`.**

**§4(e) — a settled debt's schedule stops emitting. Confirmed, and it costs nothing because it is already built.** `debtSettled(d)` is the predicate, it is called and never re-tested at a call site, and every future reader of the schedule gates on it before anything else — the bell, the card, and `PD1`. **Two additions that are mine.** Settling a debt **does not delete or blank the schedule**: it is a term of the contract, it stays true after the debt is finished exactly as the rate line does, and destroying a fact the user stated in order to tidy a display is off limits (`SCR15`). And **reversal restores nothing, because nothing was destroyed** — `SET-03`'s empty-to-undo property survives this ruling untouched.

## §6 — What the proposal is not asking for

**Accepted in full, every clause onto the off-limits list.** Not a payoff date, not an effective rate, not a projection, not a saving, not an ordering, not a new screen, not a second rate anywhere, no change to `debtOutstanding`, `debtPaid`, `debtInterestPaid`, `debtSettled`, `debtAnnualCostRate`, `debtTermDays` or any existing write path, and **not automatic payment recording — a schedule says what is owed and never marks it paid**, which this ruling enforces at `SCR6` and `SCR13` rather than merely accepting.

**Clarification, fifth outing, binding: a disclaimer in a proposal binds the proposal, not the future.** §6 is accepted for this request. It is not a prohibition on `PD1` reading the schedule when `PD1` arrives, and it will be quoted at `SCH-02` by somebody who reads the clause and not the ruling.

## Conflict Rulings

**There is no Engineering Manager report and therefore no recorded conflict list, and no reviewer findings this cycle.** These are the seven collisions I found between the proposal, the standing record and the source. Each is ruled.

### SCK1 — §2's "everything else is derivable" against §6's "not a payoff date"

**Ruling: both are right and the sentence is a trap for whoever implements this.** Deriving from a **recorded** schedule is admissible in principle — that is the whole reason a recorded schedule unlocks `PD1`. But **the last due date is a payoff date with a different label**, and the sum of instalments is one step from a saving. §2 lists them as free and they are free **arithmetically and not by permission**, which is `PK1`'s distinction arriving in the same module for the second time: availability was never the question. **Nothing derived from the schedule renders in `SCH-01` or `SCH-02`, including in a comment as an example.** `SCR9`.

### SCK2 — §2's "in `settledOn`'s shape" against what the `SET-01` narrowing actually granted

**Ruling: the narrowing is satisfied in substance and widened by exactly one shape in form, and I am saying so rather than stepping over it.** What was granted was *one **optional field** on an existing record, written only by an explicit user act, absent on every existing record, absent and explicit `null` treated identically, refused by the validator only when present and malformed, needing no migration.* A composite value meets every one of those clauses and departs from the shape the words had in view. **So the entry is narrowed a second time, by exactly one shape — a composite whose parts are meaningless apart — and the narrowing is written below so it cannot expand by association.** **Still off limits, unchanged: a collection, a storage key, a migration, a required field, a flag, a cursor, a field the application writes, an array, and nesting deeper than one level.** A constraint that opens by the width of each successive good argument is not a constraint; this one opens by the width of one object and is recorded rather than absorbed.

### SCK3 — "the largest storage change since the module shipped"

**Ruling: largest by value count, identical by risk, and genuinely new in one respect the proposal did not name.** One optional field, one validator clause, no migration, absent everywhere, one writer, one door — that is `settledOn`'s risk profile exactly. What **is** new is that it is the first stored fact whose subject is **a payment that has not happened**. I record that plainly rather than dismissing it, and Q5 is why it is admissible anyway: a term of a signed contract is a fact about the present, not a forecast. **The opening sentence is honest and it should not be quoted next time to justify a larger process than the object needs.**

### SCK4 — §4(d)'s "the first time this module would hold an expectation alongside a fact"

**Ruling: false at source, and it is what saves the request.** `totalToRepay` is the agreed and `debtPaid` is the actual; `debtOutstanding` is the subtraction; the card prints both; `dueDate` is a dated commitment about a payment that has not occurred and the chip already says *"overdue 12d"* against it. **The module has held an expectation beside a fact since it shipped, and `dueDate` is a schedule of one.** The proposal put its own possible ending in front of me and the ending does not fire — **and it was right to ask rather than assume, because if the premise had been true the answer would have been the other one.** Fifth time a proposal about this module has found the right rule and enumerated its coverage short; **first time the missing entry would have killed a request that deserved to live.**

### SCK5 — §4(c)'s "the bell would start counting instalments" against `nextPlannedDue`

**Ruling: the file already contains the answer and the proposal did not look for it.** A recurring planned expense emits **one** bell item — the next occurrence — keyed on the series. The dozen-item screen §4(c) fears is reachable only by a design nobody is obliged to choose, and Q4 forbids it before it can be chosen. **The question was still worth asking, because the answer is now a fence instead of a habit.**

### SCK6 — §2's monthly contract against the *Unverified Market Assumptions* table

**Ruling: it is a claim about the market, it decided the object's shape, and it gets a row.** `PAY-01` got **no** row because its limit was an architectural fact about the record. This is the opposite case: *"the ordinary contract is N equal monthly instalments"* is a statement about Mongolian lenders, it is the sole reason there is no frequency field, and **if it is wrong the object cannot represent a real contract and the table is what traces it.** Fourth firing of that mechanism; it has worked every time.

### SCK7 — §7's "five requests were refused for adding a model; this one stores the model's input"

**Ruling: the distinction holds, and here is exactly where it stops holding.** It holds because all three numbers are read off a document in the user's hand, which is the property `FEE-04`'s working line exists to preserve and the property the paste path had and the projection never could. **It stops the instant any one of the three is supplied by the application** — which is why `SCR3` is on the list and why it is the rejection most likely to be argued next quarter, dressed as a convenience. **The sixth debt request is the first to ship a stored fact, and it ships because it asks for the one thing five models were substituting for.**

## Architecture Strategy — next quarter

**What stays, and is not open for discussion.** Everything in the standing record and all eight design-request rulings, **with no entry narrowed by this document except the storage entry, by exactly one shape.** The single self-contained `expense-pwa\index.html` that runs by being opened from disk. No framework, no build step, no bundler, no component abstraction, **no new dependency** — reaffirmed against this request specifically: no date library, no schedule library. `localStorage`, one write seam, `load()` total, quarantine before write, numbered append-only migrations. **Offline-first, mobile-first, and correctness of financial data above everything** — and this request is decided by that last clause twice: once in refusing a derived schedule, because a stored number nobody stated is a claim nobody can check, and once in refusing a schedule that misses the agreed total by a whole instalment, because a record that cannot describe the contract should say so rather than hold three plausible numbers. A debt figure never reaches a period-filtered surface (`C38`). A collection that must never reach a total keeps its own array (`C39`). A figure the application computed by a model of its own is labelled as such (`C36`). Comments state rules, never tallies (`ARCH-01`). A citation names the thing, not the place (`C43`). **This application does not project, and a stored term of a signed contract is not a projection.**

**What changes.**

1. **The storage entry is narrowed a second time, by exactly one shape.**
   > **Permitted:** one **optional composite field on an existing record whose parts are meaningless apart**, written **only by an explicit user act**, absent on every existing record, absent and explicit `null` identical, refused by the validator only when present and malformed, needing no migration because there is nothing to transform.
   >
   > **Still off limits, unchanged and reaffirmed:** a new **collection** or storage key; a `SCHEMA_VERSION` bump; a **migration step**; a **required** field; a field written by **inference, by default, or by any path other than the user's own act**; a **stored rate**; a **status flag, boolean or cursor** on any financial record; an array; nesting deeper than one level; and a second write path into `db.debtPayments`.
2. **The record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it.** The module states what was agreed and what was paid and lets the user compare. Any figure that compares a schedule with a ledger — behind, missed, arrears, on track — is a verdict about conduct, and this application does not deliver verdicts about a user under financial stress.
3. **A deferral whose trigger is a new recorded fact is never fired by a derivation over facts already held.** Otherwise the trigger fires itself on the day it is written, and six rounds of fences turn out to have been arithmetic nobody had performed yet. **This is the rule that decided §3 and it is the most portable thing in this ruling.**
4. **A schedule contributes one reminder — the next one due — never one per occurrence.** Stated from the file's own `nextPlannedDue` precedent, binding before the first schedule reaches the bell rather than after.
5. **`dueDate` was always a schedule of one.** A module's first composite field is easier to rule when you notice that the object is a second number on a fact already stored, and this is the second consecutive ruling where the decisive move was finding the shape already in the file rather than judging the shape in the report.

**Off limits this quarter — five additions:** a schedule the application derives, infers, defaults or suggests, from any source, printed or not; any figure comparing a recorded schedule with recorded payments; a per-instalment ledger, mark or cursor on a debt record; a debt schedule in `db.planned` or in a collection of its own; and a frequency or period selector on a debt.

**Risks recorded, not scheduled. None is a finding and no reviewer raised any of them.**

- **Mine, and the largest.** This ships a fact **no screen displays**, reachable only through ✎, and its only visible consequence is `PD1`. **If `PD1` does not follow, this is storage bought for nothing.** That is the honest price of keeping four closed surfaces closed, and the owner must approve it knowing it.
- **Mine, and the sharpest.** The instalment field is one digit away from a wrong record, and the sum rule is the **only** guard — `debtProblem` cannot help, because it validates shape and a ten-times instalment is shape-valid. **If that rule is ever relaxed because a real contract did not fit, the guard is gone and a future payoff date is wrong by an order of magnitude.** The right response to a contract that does not fit is a request, not a looser rule.
- **Mine, from `SCK6`.** Monthly is baked into the object's **shape** rather than into a field. If Mongolian lenders commonly schedule fortnightly, this object cannot represent their contracts at all, and the new row in the assumptions table is what traces it back here.
- **Mine, from `SCN7`.** A user cannot tell from a card which debts carry a schedule, and cannot be told without spending the card's last closed surface.
- **Mine, from `SCK7`.** Six consecutive debt requests have produced a genuinely good reason to add a model; five were refused and the sixth ships the model's **input**. **The next request will cite this one as a precedent for storing something else, and the permitted shape above is the whole of what was granted.**
- **Carried and unchanged.** "Cost so far" still overstates an early settlement. A backup restored by an older build loses `settledOn` and now `schedule`. No archive on the Debts screen. `totalToRepay` has no edit trail. Every string is a literal and the language layer still needs its own ruling. Six bell write sites. `renderDebts` walks the payment ledger roughly seven times per debt and the pre-ruled one-`Map` fix stays unscheduled. The add form stands at ten controls and the action row at five; **this request adds to neither, deliberately.**
