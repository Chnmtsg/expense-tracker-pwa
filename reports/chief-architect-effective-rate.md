## Rulings on the Request's Six Questions

### Q1 (§3) — where the figure lands

**The conditional replacement, as proposed. (a), (b), (c) and "nowhere" are each refused, and "nowhere" is refused on its merits rather than by default.**

**Why not "nowhere", which is the answer I have to justify first.** The request invites refusal and says it would rather that than a figure that doubles without a reason. I took it seriously and it loses to the owner's own document. `knowledge\product-strategy.md` puts *"Show what the borrowing costs, in tugrik and as a rate"* second in the mission and lists **"Cost of borrowing, and the true rate"** on the permanently-free side of the line the strategy calls not a risk but a line. Round 16 refused the effective rate for one stated reason and one only — *"it is true only if the borrower repays in equal monthly installments, which the application does not record"* — and named the single day that reason expires. **That day has passed, verified at source.** Refusing now would mean the application permanently answers a smaller question than the one it was built to answer, on the exact product it was built to expose, while holding in storage the fact that makes the larger answer arithmetic. **That is not caution; it is a fence outliving its reason.**

**Why the replacement and not the three alternatives, in one line each.**

**(a) — both rates on the card. Refused permanently, `EFR1`.** *A second rate figure on a debt card, in any form, including behind a detail sheet or a disclosure* is on the off-limits list at Round 11, `ER12`, `FR4` and Round 16, and **that entry is not narrowed by this ruling by one millimetre.** The request declined to re-raise it; it is now refused against this item as well.

**(b) — the edit modal, on `PDT-01`'s precedent. Refused, `EFR2`, and the request's own reasoning is adopted and strengthened.** `PDT-01` earned that position by being a guard on `firstDue`, the one stored field nothing checks. **An effective rate guards nothing** — it reads three fields that are already guarded and catches no typo. And `PDT-01`'s own risk note said the modal is *"one good argument away from a second derived line"*. **This is that argument, the requester declined to make it, and I am closing the door behind them: `openDebtEditModal` holds exactly one derived line and the next request that wants a second gets this paragraph.**

**(c) — filling the gap where the flat rate is absent. Refused permanently, `EFR3`**, and not on the request's ground, which is weaker than it looks: the card's rate slot has meant two things selected by an optional field since `WORK-14` shipped, so *"a user with two debts would see different lines"* is the design, not the defect. **It is refused by `PDR3`, which already ruled it: suppressing the ask means the due date is never requested, so the rate the module exists to produce never becomes computable. A slot that asks for an input is not a slot that states an output.** See `EFN4` — this is not merely not-proposed, it is a trap the implementation falls into by default.

**And why the replacement is not `PDR6`. Confirmed, exactly as §3 argues it.** `PDR6`'s defect is **one label, two derivations, chosen by the presence of an optional field** — a figure whose meaning changes silently while its words stay the same. Here the words change with the derivation, each label has exactly one derivation, and the sentence names its own base. **The failure mode `PDR6` exists to prevent cannot occur. `debtTermDays` and `debtAnnualCostRate` are byte-identical after this work, and `EFR8` refuses wiring the schedule into either, by name, for the third time.**

### Q2 (§4) — "explicable on the screen where it appears"

**Satisfiable, and satisfied. The request does not end. I am ruling this at length because the request offered to end on it and deserves to know exactly what carried it.**

**First, what Round 16's clause was written about.** Its full sentence names the evil: option D would have published the lender's figure and then doubled the headline number *"with no change to the user's data and no restatement record anywhere in this application."* **Both halves of that are false here.** There is a change to the user's data — three fields they typed. And there is a restatement record: the schedule itself, permanently visible in the modal that caused the change, with `PDT-01`'s line beside it stating what it implies. **The clause is not a demand for a new mechanism. It is a demand that the change have a visible cause, and the cause is stored, editable and on screen.**

**Second, when the change happens.** It happens at the moment of the user's own act, on the screen they return to from that act, seconds after it, and it is reversible by emptying the same three fields. **That is the best moment a change of this kind can have**, and it is not the moment option D would have had, which was a version upgrade.

**Third, and this is the part the request could not have, because it follows from the model correction at Q4.** Under the corrected model the two sentences are annualisations of **the same two stored amounts over the same contract**: `totalToRepay − principal`, the tugrik cost, appears unchanged in both. **The number moves because the question moved, not because the money did.** That is a sentence a user can be given, and under this ruling it is given — in the wording, at Q1's shape, not in a fourth surface.

**So the ruling is the first of the request's three shapes: the sentence carries its own explanation in its wording and nothing else is said.** The second shape — a one-time disclosure at the moment of change — is refused permanently at `EFR5`, and not on taste: *"once and never again"* requires remembering that it was shown, which is a stored flag on a financial record or a new storage key, and both are off limits at exactly the width they were granted. **A new mechanism for one sentence is the speculation this record exists to refuse.** The third shape — ship it unexplained and record a risk — is refused at `EFR6`: a risk note is not an explanation and this ruling is not entitled to buy a figure with a paragraph nobody reads.

**And the wording is where that answer is spent, so the wording is ruled here and is load-bearing** — the third consecutive ruling in this module to say that about a sentence, and the reason is at `EFN9`.

### Q3 (§5's first note) — is the on-screen assumption disclosure discharged?

**Discharged. But not for the reason the request gives, and not by itself — it is discharged because it has been converted into a refusal, and if I had ruled it still owed this request would have ended, because the card's rate copy is closed at one sentence and there is nowhere for a disclosure to live.** The request is owed that said plainly.

**The request's reason is right as far as it goes:** the assumption Round 16 wrote that clause about — *equal instalments* — is now `d.schedule`, typed by the user. Correct.

**Its residual is wrong in one direction and incomplete in the other.**

It says what remains assumed is *"only that the payments happen when the schedule says."* **That is not an assumption at all, and saying so is the useful half of this answer.** The effective rate is a property of the **contract**, exactly as `debtAnnualCostRate` is, and the file already states the rule beside it: *"the rate is a property of the contract, so it stays true after the debt is settled."* A rate over agreed terms is what the agreement costs if performed; it forecasts nothing, it asserts nothing about conduct, and it is exactly as true if the user pays nothing. **`PDK2` decided this for a date and it decides it again for a percentage: this application does not project, and a figure derived from the terms of a signed contract is not a projection.** `EFK8`.

**What the request missed is a second assumption, and it is the one that mattered.** Its model places the first payment one month after the money arrived. **The record can contradict that**, because it stores `date` and `firstDue` separately and nothing bounds the gap. On §2's row 2 a first payment two months out instead of one moves the figure from **81.2% to 66.4%** — fifteen points, on the headline figure, silently, in the understating direction. **That is an assumption the application would be making about a contract while holding the fact that refutes it.** It is removed at Q4 rather than disclosed, and its removal is what actually discharges Round 16's clause.

**So: no disclosure sentence, anywhere. No "if you pay as agreed", which is a warning about conduct and is `SCR6`'s and `PDR5`'s shape in miniature. No second sentence on the card in any form.** The assumption Round 16 wanted stated is now a stored fact, and the two it did not anticipate are handled by refusal — `null`, and the flat sentence in its place.

### Q4 (§5's second note) — the solver's inputs

**Corrected, not confirmed — and the correction makes the function smaller, not larger.** The request asked me to confirm or correct, and the honest answer is correct, in two places.

**What is confirmed.** Principal out at day zero, from `d.principal`. Cash in from the schedule and never from `db.debtPayments`. Pure over one debt record. No `db` read. These are right and they are binding.

**Correction one: the last payment is anchored on `totalToRepay`, not on `instalment`.** The request's ground for excluding `totalToRepay` is that *"the sum rule permits `instalment × count` to differ from it by up to one instalment, and the schedule is the thing being modelled."* **That sentence contains its own refutation.** The tolerance is **one whole instalment**, not one tugrik — the request conflated the sum rule's tolerance with the whole-tugrik rounding of an exact instalment, and the two differ by four orders of magnitude. A schedule of ₮430,000 × 3 against a stored total of ₮1,300,000 passes the boundary and is a perfectly ordinary record whose real last payment is ₮440,000. **Modelling it as three payments of ₮430,000 drops ₮10,000 of the agreed cost and reports 376.6% where the truth is 400.4% — twenty-four points, understating.** `SCH-02`'s own ruling says what that record is: *"N equal instalments covering an agreed total, **with the remainder inside one of them**."* So the model puts it where the contract does.

> **Payments 1 through `count − 1` are `instalment`. Payment `count` is `totalToRepay − instalment × (count − 1)`. The cash flows sum to `totalToRepay` exactly, by construction.**

**And this is what makes Q2's answer available**: both sentences on the card now annualise the same stored cost, so the number changes and the money does not.

**Correction two: the periods are the dated payments, measured in days, not twelve equal twelfths.** This removes the fifteen-point error at Q3 and it is not a new convention — it is the module's own, written in `debtAnnualCostRate`'s comment in capitals: *"DAYS, NEVER MONTHS. Converting the stored dates to a whole month count needs a rounding rule… That error runs downward on short terms, which are the expensive ones. 365 fixed."* **A function that sat directly beneath that paragraph and modelled twelve equal months would contradict its own neighbour.** `feeTermMonths` uses months for a stated reason that does not reach here — months are what *"3% сарын хүү"* means, and there is no lender-quoted monthly charge in this derivation, only dated cash flows.

> **The payment dates are `firstDue` walked `count − 1` times through `stepDate`'s `'monthly'` branch with `anchorDay` from `firstDue` — the same walk `debtPayoffDate` makes, through the same engine, and there is no second engine. Each date becomes a day offset from `d.date` in the module's existing idiom. The solve is for a daily rate and the annualisation is `(1 + r) ** 365 − 1`, 365 fixed, no leap correction, on the constant already stated one function above.**

**The cost of the correction is that §2's and §4's published figures shift upward by roughly half a point**, because real months are not equal twelfths and the payments land slightly earlier on average than a twelfth-of-a-year model assumes. **I am amending Round 16's fixture condition accordingly rather than stepping over it** — see `EFF-01`(8) and `EFN3`. The four rows remain the regression; they are derived in the flow from stated cash flows rather than copied from a table computed under a model this ruling does not ship, and the decoder's table is recorded beside them as the corroboration it is.

### Q5 — `DEBT_RATE_DISPLAY_MAX`

**Unchanged at 1,000. One constant, two readers, its comment amended in the same commit to state the rule over both. Not raised, not lowered, not duplicated, and no second ceiling for the second figure. `EFR7`.**

**The request's worry is that the cap was ruled for a figure with roughly half this range. That is true and it is an argument for the cap, not against it.** The constant's own comment states its ground and the ground is a property of the reader, not of the derivation: *"A payday loan over three days annualises past 1,200%, which is arithmetically correct and reads to an untrained user as a broken application — discrediting every other figure on the screen, including the right ones."* **A wider range means the ceiling fires more often, which is the ceiling working.** Raising it would be raising it toward the number that reads as broken, on the screen belonging to somebody under financial stress, in the module whose entire honesty argument is that the label carries the figure.

**A second, higher constant for the effective rate is refused for the same reason plus one more:** two display ceilings for two sentences in one slot are two things to keep in step, and the first time they drift the card prints a bare figure beside a capped one with no rule anybody can state.

**And the property that was ruled at Round 16 is reaffirmed at full width: it bounds what is printed and never what is computed.** `EFR13` refuses a clamped derivation by name, the existing harness flow *"an extreme rate is capped in what it prints, not in what it computes"* stays green and unmodified, and the new flows assert the same property for the new reader.

**EFN8 — the sentence is the first card-visible consequence of a schedule, and that is incidental, not an answer to `PDD1`.** It appears only where a due date and a cost already exist, it never names the schedule, and a user with no schedule learns nothing about why another card reads differently. **Recorded so nobody cites `EFF-02` as having discharged a deferral whose trigger is an observation.**

**EFN9 — the owner's stated market fact decides the wording, and the request's draft contradicts it.** *"Simple interest on the original amount, stated by the product owner: 3% of what was borrowed, every month, regardless of what is left"* is in `feeComputeTotal`'s comment and in `product-strategy.md`'s assumptions table. **A sentence reading *"81% a year on the money you still owe"* asserts that this lender charges on the falling balance, which the owner has said they do not.** The ruled wording states an equivalence — *"as much as a loan charging…"* — which is true, makes no claim about the contract, and is the safest framing available. **Fourth time this module has recorded that the wording and not the arithmetic is the load-bearing part.**

**EFN10 — an existing harness flow becomes breakable by accident.** *"The yearly cost line names what the percentage is a share of"* asserts the flat sentence over a record with no schedule. **It must stay green and unmodified**, and the temptation to "update" it when the effective sentence lands is the way the flat sentence's wording guard gets quietly disarmed — which has happened once before in this module, to a `WORK-08` assertion, and is recorded in `HANDOFF.md`.

## Architecture Strategy — next quarter

**What stays, and is not open for discussion.** Everything in the standing record and all ten design-request rulings, **with no entry narrowed by this document except the rate-model entry, by exactly one shape, at `EFK1`.** The single self-contained `expense-pwa\index.html` that runs by being opened from disk. No framework, no build step, no bundler, no component abstraction, **no new dependency — reaffirmed against this request specifically: no date library, no schedule library and no finance library, because `stepDate` is the one definition of a recurrence step and a second one has cost this repository twice.** `localStorage`, one write seam, `load()` total, quarantine before write, numbered append-only migrations. **Offline-first, mobile-first, and correctness of financial data above everything** — and this request is decided by that last clause three times: once in approving a figure the record can now support, once in correcting a model that would have understated by fifteen points and twenty-four, and once in refusing a sentence that would have stated the terms of a contract the application has never seen. A debt figure never reaches a period-filtered surface (`C38`). A collection that must never reach a total keeps its own array (`C39`). A figure the application computed by a model of its own is labelled as such (`C36`). Comments state rules, never tallies (`ARCH-01`). A citation names the thing, not the place (`C43`). **The record holds the agreement; the application never scores performance against it.**

**What changes.**

1. **A figure may be replaced by a better-founded figure in the same slot, when the record acquires the fact the better one needs — and the replacement is one sentence, never two.** The rule that makes it safe is that **each label has exactly one derivation**. `PDR6` refuses one label with two; it never refused two labels with one each, and the distinction is the most portable thing in this ruling.
2. **When a model's assumption can be contradicted by a fact the record already holds, remove the assumption rather than disclose it.** The disclosure Round 16 pre-ruled was unshippable on a card closed at one sentence; measuring the actual dates was smaller than a disclosure and smaller than a window constant. **A convention is only cheaper than a measurement when the measurement is unavailable.**
3. **DAYS, NEVER MONTHS is now a module rule with two readers rather than one function's note.** A month count needs a rounding rule, and every rounding rule in this module runs downward on short terms, which are the predatory ones.
4. **An application may say what a borrowing costs; it may not say what a lender charges.** Where a figure could be read as a term of the contract, the sentence frames an equivalence. **This is `C36` sharpened by a market fact the owner stated and the repository does not know.**
5. **A deferral that fires is discharged, not re-tabled — second consecutive application.** Round 16's effective-rate row becomes two items and leaves the table; the residual hazard it exposed becomes `EFD1` with its own trigger rather than surviving inside a discharged row.

**Off limits this quarter — five additions:** a second rate figure on a debt card in any form, including any comparison between the two sentences; a one-time or dismissible disclosure mechanism anywhere in this application; a whole-month term derivation for any stored-record figure; the words "effective", "true rate", "real rate" or "interest rate" on any screen; and a cached, memoised or stored value of any derived rate.

**Risks recorded, not scheduled. None is a finding and no reviewer raised any of them.**

- **Mine, and the largest.** **A `firstDue` typed a year wrong now moves the card's headline percentage by tens of points, downward, silently.** `PDT-01`'s line shows it and does not stop it; `EFR17` refuses the only cross-check available; `EFD1` carries it with a trigger. **The direction is understatement, which is the direction this module is least able to detect in itself.**
- **Mine, and the sharpest.** **The wording is doing more work here than in any sentence this module has shipped.** *"As much as a loan charging X"* is what keeps the figure from asserting the lender's terms. **Weaken it to "on the money you still owe" and the application is stating a contract it has never seen; weaken it to "the interest rate" and it is stating one it has been told is false.** The harness asserts the words for exactly that reason, as it does for the flat sentence.
- **Mine, from `EFN8`.** **The sentence is the first card-visible consequence of a schedule and it is not a discoverability answer.** A user who has not opened ✎ sees no reason to. `PDD1` is unmoved and somebody will cite this ruling as having closed it.
- **Mine, from `EFN7`.** **The file now performs a numeric solve inside a render loop.** Bounded, cheap at this scale, and the first of its kind. If it ever bites, the answer is the pre-ruled `Map`, not a cached rate.
- **Mine, from `EFK6`.** **Two published tables of the same four rows now disagree in one figure, and a third set of numbers ships in the harness.** The fixture derives rather than copies, and the flow header states the derivation, which is the only version of this that survives a decimal.
- **Carried and unchanged.** The instalment field is one digit away from a wrong record and the sum rule is the only guard — **and that guard now protects a headline percentage as well as a stored object.** Monthly is baked into the schedule's shape rather than a field. "Cost so far" still overstates an early settlement. A backup restored by an older build loses `settledOn` and `schedule`. No archive on the Debts screen. `totalToRepay` has no edit trail and is now load-bearing for one more figure. Every string is a literal and the language layer still needs its own ruling. Six bell write sites. `renderDebts` walks the payment ledger roughly seven times per debt. The add form stands at ten controls and the action row at five; **this request adds to neither, deliberately.**

## Final Recommendation

**Take `EFF-01` alone first: one function in the derived-figures block beside `debtAnnualCostRate`, pure over one debt record, that returns `null` on anything it does not like — no schedule, a count below two or above `DEBT_SCHEDULE_MONTHS_MAX`, a `firstDue` that is not ISO or is earlier than the borrow date, a cost of zero or less, instalments that miss the agreed total by a whole instalment, a last payment of zero or less, or a bracket that fails to straddle — and otherwise bisects, forty fixed iterations, for the daily rate that discounts the schedule's dated cash flows back to the principal, and annualises it at 365. The payments are `instalment` for all but the last and `totalToRepay − instalment × (count − 1)` for the last, so they sum to the agreed total exactly; the dates come from `stepDate`'s monthly branch with `anchorDay` from `firstDue`, and the offsets are days from the borrow date. `PDN6`'s correction rides that commit.
